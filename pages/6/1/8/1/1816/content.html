Idea

The effective topos EffEff is an example of an elementary topos with a natural numbers object which is not a Grothendieck topos (and doesn’t even have a geometric morphism to Set).

It is an environment for higher order recursion theory, where, in the internal logic, it is provable that every total function from natural numbers to natural numbers is recursive (furthermore, the functor Hom(1,−)Hom(1, -) from the effective topos into Set, the inverse image part of a geometric morphism Set→EffSet \to Eff preserves the natural numbers object, giving a suitable version of this result in the external logic as well).

It can be specified as the realizability topos for Kleene's first algebra.

The effective topos construction alluded in the above paragraph can be performed more generally, in every topos EE with a natural numbers object, replacing Set. To every such topos one constructs the corresponding “external” effective topos eEe E and the correspondence E↦eEE \mapsto e E extends to a functor admitting a fully faithful right adjoint. Kleene’s first algebra can also be replaced by any partial combinatory algebra, or even some more general types of gadgets; toposes obtained in this way are called realizability toposes.

The effective topos is the category obtained from the category of sets by first freely adjoining recursively-indexed coproducts (but being careful to preserve the empty set), and then adding quotients of (pseudo-)equivalence relations. (RobinsonRosolini).

Definition

An object of the effective topos is a set XX along with an effective PER E X⊆ℕ×X×XE_X \subseteq \mathbb{N} \times X \times X. This means that (p 2,3) *(E X)⊆X×X(p_{2,3})_*(E_X) \subseteq X \times X is a partial equivalence relation on XX, with “computational proofs” of the symmetry and transitivity properties. In particular, let us use notation n⊩x≡yn \Vdash x \equiv y for (n,x,y)∈E X(n, x, y) \in E_X. What we require is:

  • (Symmetry tracker) There exists a partial recursive function s X:ℕ⤏ℕs_X : \mathbb{N} \dashrightarrow \mathbb{N} such that whenever

    n⊩x≡y,n \Vdash x \equiv y,

    then s X(n)s_X(n) is defined and

    s X(n)⊩y≡x.s_X(n) \Vdash y \equiv x.

  • (Transitivity tracker) There exists a partial recursive function t X:ℕ 2⤏ℕt_X : \mathbb{N}^2 \dashrightarrow \mathbb{N} such that whenever

    n⊩x≡yn \Vdash x \equiv y

    and

    m⊩y≡z,m \Vdash y \equiv z,

    then t X(n,m)t_X(n, m) is defined and

    t X(n,m)⊩x≡z.t_X(n, m) \Vdash x \equiv z.

As for morphisms, they will be defined by equivalence classes of effective functional relations under a certain equivalence relation. In particular, an effective functional relation between objects (X,E X)(X, E_X) and (Y,E Y)(Y, E_Y) will be a subset f⊆ℕ×X×Yf \subseteq \mathbb{N} \times X \times Y such that using notation n⊩x↦fyn \Vdash x \xmapsto{f} y for (n,x,y)∈f(n, x, y) \in f, we have:

  • (Domain tracker) There exists a partial recursive function d f:ℕ⤏ℕd_f : \mathbb{N} \dashrightarrow \mathbb{N} such that whenever

    n⊩x↦fy,n \Vdash x \xmapsto{f} y,

    then d f(n)d_f(n) is defined and

    d f(n)⊩x≡x.d_f(n) \Vdash x \equiv x.

  • (Codomain tracker) There exists a partial recursive function c f:ℕ⤏ℕc_f : \mathbb{N} \dashrightarrow \mathbb{N} such that whenever

    n⊩x↦fy,n \Vdash x \xmapsto{f} y,

    then c f(n)c_f(n) is defined and

    c f(n)⊩y≡y.c_f(n) \Vdash y \equiv y.

  • (Image uniqueness tracker) There exists a partial recursive function u f:ℕ 2⤏ℕu_f : \mathbb{N}^2 \dashrightarrow \mathbb{N} such that whenever

    n⊩x↦fy 1n \Vdash x \xmapsto{f} y_1

    and

    m⊩x↦fy 2,m \Vdash x \xmapsto{f} y_2,

    then u f(n,m)u_f(n, m) is defined and

    u f(n,m)⊩y 1≡y 2.u_f(n, m) \Vdash y_1 \equiv y_2.

  • (Totality tracker) There exists a partial recursive function t f:ℕ⤏ℕt_f : \mathbb{N} \dashrightarrow \mathbb{N} such that whenever

    n⊩x≡x,n \Vdash x \equiv x,

    then t f(n)t_f(n) is defined, and there exists y∈Yy\in Y such that

    t f(n)⊩x↦fy.t_f(n) \Vdash x \xmapsto{f} y.

  • (Respectfulness tracker) There exists a partial recursive function r f:ℕ 3⤏ℕr_f : \mathbb{N}^3 \dashrightarrow \mathbb{N} such that whenever

    n⊩x 1↦fy 1,n \Vdash x_1 \xmapsto{f} y_1,
    m⊩x 1≡x 2,m \Vdash x_1 \equiv x_2,

    and

    p⊩y 1≡y 2,p \Vdash y_1 \equiv y_2,

    then r f(n,m,p)r_f(n, m, p) is defined and

    r f(n,m,p)⊩x 2↦fy 2.r_f(n, m, p) \Vdash x_2 \xmapsto{f} y_2.

As for the equivalence relation, we will say that effective functional relations f,g⊆ℕ×X×Yf, g \subseteq \mathbb{N} \times X \times Y are equivalent if there exists an equivalence tracker, i.e. a partial recursive function e f,g:ℕ⤏ℕe_{f,g} : \mathbb{N} \dashrightarrow \mathbb{N} such that whenever

n⊩x↦fy,n \Vdash x \xmapsto{f} y,

then e f,g(n)e_{f,g}(n) is defined, and

e f,g(n)⊩x↦gy.e_{f,g}(n) \Vdash x \xmapsto{g} y.

For the other pieces of data required to make this a category, first define the identity morphism on XX to be the equivalence class of E XE_X, i.e. the equivalence class of the effective functional relation id X\operatorname{id}_X where n⊩x↦id Xyn \Vdash x \xmapsto{\operatorname{id}_X} y if and only if n⊩x≡yn \Vdash x \equiv y.

As for compositions, this will be induced by the following composition operation on effective functional relations: suppose gg is an effective functional relation between (X,E X)(X, E_X) and (Y,E Y)(Y, E_Y), and ff is an effective functional relation between (Y,E Y)(Y, E_Y) and (Z,E Z)(Z, E_Z). Also, fix a full recursive pairing bijection ⟨−,−⟩:ℕ 2→ℕ\langle {-}, {-} \rangle : \mathbb{N}^2 \to \mathbb{N} with full recursive projections p 1,p 2:ℕ⇉ℕp_1, p_2 : \mathbb{N} \rightrightarrows \mathbb{N} such that (p 1,p 2):ℕ→ℕ 2(p_1, p_2) : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}^2 is the inverse of ⟨−,−⟩\langle {-}, {-} \rangle. Then f∘g⊆ℕ×X×Zf \circ g \subseteq \mathbb{N} \times X \times Z will be the effective functional relation consisting of triples (n,x,z)(n, x, z) such that there exists y∈Yy\in Y with

p 1(n)⊩x↦gyp_1(n) \Vdash x \xmapsto{g} y

and

p 2(n)⊩y↦fz.p_2(n) \Vdash y \xmapsto{f} z.
