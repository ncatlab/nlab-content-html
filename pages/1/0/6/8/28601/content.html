Idea

Donaldson invariants, due to Donaldson 1986, 1990, are invariants of the smooth structure of smooth 4-manifolds based on the methods behind Donaldson's theorem, first described by Simon Donaldson in 1983 and improved in 1987. In particular, key informations are obtained from the Yang-Mills moduli space of the Yang-Mills equations on the 4-manifold, which as partial differential equations depend on the smooth structure. Donaldson invariants can distinguish these, hence are able to detect exotic smooth structures.

SU(2) Donaldson invariants

Let XX be an oriented closed smooth 4-manifold and P↠XP\twoheadrightarrow X be a principal SU(2)-bundle, then there exists a unique homotopy class represented by a classifying map τ:X→BSU(2)≅ℍP ∞\tau\colon X\rightarrow BSU(2)\cong\mathbb{H}P^\infty into the classifying space for SU(2) giving a pullback P=τ *ESU(2)=τ *S ∞P=\tau^*ESU(2)=\tau^*S^\infty. With the Kronecker pairing and the fundamental class [X]∈H 4(X,ℤ)[X]\in H_4(X,\mathbb{Z}) from the orientation, the second Chern class concretely gives a group isomorphism:

⟨c 2(−),[X]⟩:Prin SU(2)(X)→≅ℤ. \langle c_2(-),[X]\rangle\colon Prin_{SU(2)}(X)\xrightarrow\cong\mathbb{Z}.

Let 𝒜:=Ω Ad 1(P,𝔰𝔲(2))\mathcal{A}:=\Omega_{Ad}^1(P,\mathfrak{su}(2)) be the configuration space of connections on PP, which is an affine vector space not canonically isomorphic to Ω 1(X,Ad(P))\Omega^1(X,Ad(P)). Let 𝒢≔Aut SU(2)(P)\mathcal{G}\coloneqq Aut_{SU(2)}(P) be the gauge group of gauge transformations of PP, which acts on 𝒜\mathcal{A}. Let ℬ≔𝒜/𝒢\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{G} be the orbit space, then there is a weak homotopy equivalence ℬ→Map P(X,BSU(2))\mathcal{B}\rightarrow Map_P(X,BSU(2)) in the homotopy class of the classifying map of PP. Currying it corresponds to a map ℬ×X→BSU(2)\mathcal{B}\times X\rightarrow BSU(2) and through pullback a principal SU(2)SU(2)-bundle P˜↠ℬ×X\widetilde{P}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{B}\times X. For every gauge class [A]∈ℬ[A]\in\mathcal{B}, one has P˜| {[A]}×X≅P↠X\widetilde{P}\vert_{\{[A]\}\times X}\cong P\twoheadrightarrow X. With the second Chern class c 2(P˜)∈H 4(ℬ×X,ℤ)c_2(\widetilde{P})\in H^4(\mathcal{B}\times X,\mathbb{Z}), the slant product yields a map:

μ:H 2(X,ℤ)→H 2(ℬ,ℤ),μ(α)≔c 2(P˜)/α. \mu\colon H_2(X,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^2(\mathcal{B},\mathbb{Z}), \mu(\alpha)\coloneqq c_2(\widetilde{P})/\alpha.

With the subset ℳ⊂ℬ\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{B} of an (anti) self-dual Yang-Mills moduli space there is an induced map H 2(ℬ,ℤ)↪H 2(ℳ,ℤ)H^2(\mathcal{B},\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow H^2(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{Z}), which through postcomposition yields a map μ:H 2(X,ℤ)→H 2(ℳ,ℤ)\mu\colon H_2(X,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^2(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{Z}). With the interpretation of the homology classes as embedded surfaces i:Σ↪Mi\colon\Sigma\hookrightarrow M with i *[Σ]∈H 2(M,ℤ)i_*[\Sigma]\in H_2(M,\mathbb{Z}) and the interpretation of the cohomology classes as differential forms of second order using de Rham cohomology H 2(ℳ,ℝ)≅H dR 2(ℳ)H^2(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{R})\cong H_{dR}^2(\mathcal{M}), one can see the map μ\mu as mapping embedded surfaces in MM to differential forms of second order in ℳ\mathcal{M}. Donaldson invariants are defined using the integration of these differential forms, which is disrupted by the missing compactness and the singularities of the moduli space. A compactification ℳ⊂ℳ¯\mathcal{M}\subset\overline\mathcal{M} can be constructed using Uhlenbeck's compactness theorem and leads to a ℤ\mathbb{Z}-linear map μ:H 2(X,ℤ)→H 2(ℳ¯,ℤ)\mu\colon H_2(X,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^2(\overline\mathcal{M},\mathbb{Z}).

Let P k↠XP_k\twoheadrightarrow X be the unique principal SU(2)-bundle with ⟨c 2(P k),[X]⟩=k\langle c_2(P_k),[X]\rangle=k according to the above group isomorphism, then ⟨p 1Ad(P k),[X]⟩=−4k\langle p_1 Ad(P_k),[X]\rangle=-4k for the first Pontrjagin class of the adjoint bundle and the virtual dimension of its anti self-dual Yang-Mills (ASDYM) moduli space is given with the Atiyah-Singer index theorem by:

dimℳ k=−8k−3(1−b 1(M)+b 2 +(M)). \dim\mathcal{M}_k =-8k-3(1-b_1(M)+b_2^+(M)).

If −b 1(M)+b 2 +(M)-b_1(M)+b_2^+(M) is odd, then the virtual dimension is an even number dimℳ k=2d k\dim\mathcal{M}_k=2d_k, potentially negative given the choice of kk. With a fundamental class [ℳ k]∈H 2d k(ℳ k,ℤ)≅ℤ b 0(ℳ k)\left[\mathcal{M}_k\right]\in H_{2d_k}(\mathcal{M}_k,\mathbb{Z}) \cong\mathbb{Z}^{b_0\left(\mathcal{M}_k\right)} since the ASDYM moduli space is orientable, the Donaldson invariant is given with the cup product and the Kronecker pairing:

𝒟 X,k:Sym d kH 2(X,ℤ)→ℤ,𝒟 X,k(α 1,…,α d k)≔⟨μ(α 1)⌣…⌣μ(α d k),[ℳ k]⟩. \mathcal{D}_{X,k}\colon Sym^{d_k}H_2(X,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}, \mathcal{D}_{X,k}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{d_k})\coloneqq\left\langle\mu(\alpha_1)\smile\ldots\smile\mu(\alpha_{d_k}), \left[\mathcal{M}_k\right]\right\rangle.

Considering the second homology classes in a symmetric setting only up to permutation comes from the symmetry of the cup product, hence the irrelevance of any order. It can furthermore be shown, that the Donaldson invariants also don’t depend on the Riemannian metric and therefore only on the smooth structure.

Change of instanton number

According to the above formula, dimℳ k+1=dimℳ k+8\dim\mathcal{M}_{k+1}=\dim\mathcal{M}_k+8 holds under a change of the instanton number, expanding the input of the Donaldson invariant by four new second homology classes. More generally, the slant product yields a ℤ\mathbb{Z}-linear map μ:H n(X,ℤ)→H 4−n(ℳ¯,ℤ)\mu\colon H_n(X,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{4-n}(\overline\mathcal{M},\mathbb{Z}), so for XX connected with H 0(X,ℤ)≅ℤH_0(X,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z} a map μ:ℤ→H 4(ℳ¯,ℤ)\mu\colon\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow H^4(\overline\mathcal{M},\mathbb{Z}). Hence one can also expand the Donaldson invariant by two images of a generator.

SO(3) Donaldson invariants

Let P↠XP\twoheadrightarrow X be a principal SO(3)-bundle, then there exists a unique homotopy class represented by a classifying map τ:X→BSO(3)\tau\colon X\rightarrow BSO(3) into the classifying space for SO(3) giving a pullback P=τ *ESO(3)P=\tau^* ESO(3). Now the second Stiefel-Whitney class and the first Pontrjagin class give a group isomorphism:

(w 2,⟨p 1(−),[X]⟩):Prin SO(3)(X)→≅{(v,n)∈H 2(X,ℤ 2)×ℤ|⟨v 2,[X]⟩≡nmod4}. (w_2,\langle p_1(-),[X]\rangle)\colon Prin_{SO(3)}(X)\xrightarrow\cong\{(v,n)\in H^2(X,\mathbb{Z}_2)\times\mathbb{Z}|\langle v^2,[X]\rangle\equiv n mod 4\}.

In this case, the first Pontrjagin class replaces the second Chern class in the slant product.

Donaldson invariants of compactification

Compactification to two dimensions

According to the classification of surfaces, every oriented closed 2-manifold (surface) is homeomorphic to a Sigma surface Σ g=#gT 2\Sigma_g=\# gT^2, given as a connected sum of 2-tori T 2=S 1×S 1T^2=S^1\times S^1 with the convention Σ 0=S 2\Sigma_0=S^2. Now the Donaldson invariants can be considered on the 4-manifold Σ g×S 2\Sigma_g\times S^2.

Compactification to three dimensions

For an oriented closed 3-manifold YY, one can consider the Donaldson invariants on the 4-manifold Y×S 1Y\times S^1. For general structure groups, they relate to the Rozansky-Witten invariants of YY and for the structure group SU(2)SU(2), they relate to the Casson-Walker-Lescop invariant? of YY.
