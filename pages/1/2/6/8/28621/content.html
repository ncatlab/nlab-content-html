Idea

A group presentation may be realized homotopy-theoretically by attaching 2-cells to a wedge sum of circles along loops representing the defining relations.

In homotopy type theory, this construction is expressed by a homotopy pushout. Its 1-truncation gives a model for the classifying space of the presented group.

This construction relates the algebraic description of a group by generators and relations to its description as the fundamental group of a connected space.

Construction

A group presentation can be realized geometrically by representing generators as loops and relations as homotopies contracting specified compositions of these loops. The resulting space is constructed by attaching 2-cells to a wedge sum of circles, or equivalently by forming a homotopy pushout.

Let XX be a set of generators and RR a set of relators, equipped with a function r:R→F(X)r:R\to F(X), where F(X)F(X) is the free group on XX. The presented group is

G P:=F(X)/⟨⟨r(R)⟩⟩, G_P := F(X)/\langle\!\langle r(R)\rangle\!\rangle,

where ⟨⟨r(R)⟩⟩\langle\!\langle r(R)\rangle\!\rangle denotes the normal closure of the relators. The construction below realizes this quotient at the level of fundamental groups.

The generator space

Consider the pointed space

W X:=⋁ x∈XS 1, W_X := \bigvee_{x\in X} S^1,

the wedge sum of circles indexed by XX, with all circles identified at a common basepoint. Each generator x∈Xx\in X determines a distinguished based loop γ x:S 1→W X\gamma_x:S^1\to W_X.

By the Seifert-van Kampen theorem, the fundamental group of this space is the free group on XX:

π 1(W X)≅F(X). \pi_1(W_X)\cong F(X).

Under this isomorphism, the homotopy class [γ x][\gamma_x] corresponds to the free generator xx. Group multiplication is represented by concatenation of loops, while inversion corresponds to reversing their orientation. For instance, the word x 1x 2 −1x 3x_1x_2^{-1}x_3 is represented by the composite loop γ x 1⋅γ x 2 −1⋅γ x 3\gamma_{x_1}\cdot\gamma_{x_2}^{-1}\cdot\gamma_{x_3}.

Thus, W XW_X realizes the generators without imposing any relations.

Attaching the relations

Each relator a∈Ra\in R determines an element r(a)∈F(X)≅π 1(W X)r(a)\in F(X)\cong\pi_1(W_X), represented by a based loop ρ a:S 1→W X\rho_a:S^1\to W_X.

To impose the relation r(a)=1r(a)=1, one attaches a disk D 2D^2 along ρ a\rho_a. The disk provides a null-homotopy of the corresponding loop, making its homotopy class trivial in the resulting space.

Collecting these loops gives an attaching map ρ:R×S 1→W X\rho:R\times S^1\to W_X, defined by ρ(a,z)=ρ a(z)\rho(a,z)=\rho_a(z). The presentation space is the homotopy pushout

C P:=hocolim(W X←ρR×S 1→pr 1R). C_P := \operatorname{hocolim} \left( W_X \xleftarrow{\rho} R\times S^1 \xrightarrow{\mathrm{pr}_1} R \right).

Here pr 1:R×S 1→R\mathrm{pr}_1:R\times S^1\to R sends each circle to its indexing relator. Equivalently, the construction attaches one 2-cell for every relator:

C P≃W X∪ ∐ a∈RS 1∐ a∈RD 2. C_P\simeq W_X\cup_{\coprod_{a\in R}S^1} \coprod_{a\in R}D^2.

This is the classical presentation CW-complex. The homotopy pushout retains the homotopy data of the attachments, whereas an ordinary pushout that simply collapses each relator loop may fail to preserve the intended homotopy type.

In homotopy type theory, the same construction can be expressed using a pushout type, whose path constructors encode the homotopies associated with the attachments.

Example: a cyclic group

Consider the presentation G=⟨x∣x n=1⟩≅ℤ/nℤG = \langle x \mid x^n = 1 \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, for n≥1n\geq 1.

Since there is one generator, W X=S 1W_X=S^1. The relator x nx^n is represented by the degree-nn map ρ n:S 1→S 1\rho_n:S^1\to S^1, and the presentation space is

C P=S 1∪ ρ nD 2. C_P=S^1\cup_{\rho_n}D^2.

The attached disk makes the loop traversing the circle nn times null-homotopic. By the Seifert-van Kampen theorem,

π 1(C P)≅⟨x∣x n=1⟩≅ℤ/nℤ \pi_1(C_P) \cong \langle x \mid x^n = 1 \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}

This illustrates the general correspondence: generators determine loops, relators determine 2-cell attachments, and the resulting fundamental group is the group specified by the presentation.

Fundamental group

The fundamental group of the presentation space C PC_P is precisely the group specified by the original presentation. This follows from the Seifert-van Kampen theorem, which describes how attaching 2-cells affects the fundamental group of a space.

Recall that the generator space W X=⋁ x∈XS 1W_X=\bigvee_{x\in X}S^1 satisfies π 1(W X)≅F(X)\pi_1(W_X)\cong F(X). Each relator a∈Ra\in R determines a loop ρ a:S 1→W X\rho_a:S^1\to W_X, whose homotopy class corresponds to r(a)∈F(X)r(a)\in F(X).

Attaching a 2-cell along ρ a\rho_a makes this loop null-homotopic. At the level of fundamental groups, this imposes the relation r(a)=1r(a)=1. Moreover, every conjugate wr(a)w −1wr(a)w^{-1} also becomes trivial, since the induced map on fundamental groups is a group homomorphism.

Consequently, the kernel of the homomorphism induced by the inclusion W X→C PW_X\to C_P is exactly the normal closure of the relators:

ker(π 1(W X)→π 1(C P))=⟨⟨r(R)⟩⟩. \ker\bigl(\pi_1(W_X)\to\pi_1(C_P)\bigr) = \langle\!\langle r(R)\rangle\!\rangle.

By the Seifert-van Kampen theorem, the induced homomorphism is surjective, and therefore

π 1(C P)≅F(X)/⟨⟨r(R)⟩⟩≅G P. \pi_1(C_P) \cong F(X)/\langle\!\langle r(R)\rangle\!\rangle \cong G_P.

Thus, the homotopy pushout realizes the algebraic quotient defining the presented group. The generators become homotopy classes of loops, while the relations become null-homotopies of the corresponding composite loops.

This construction is valid for arbitrary sets of generators and relators, without requiring the presentation to be finite.

Classifying space

Although C PC_P has fundamental group G PG_P, it is not necessarily a classifying space for G PG_P. A classifying space BG PBG_P, regarded as an Eilenberg-Mac Lane space K(G P,1)K(G_P,1), has fundamental group G PG_P and trivial higher homotopy groups.

The presentation space C PC_P is a connected CW-complex of dimension at most two, but it may have nontrivial higher homotopy groups. In particular, the relations imposed on its fundamental group do not necessarily eliminate higher-dimensional homotopy information.

To obtain the classifying space, consider the 1-truncation of C PC_P, denoted by ‖C P‖ 1\|C_P\|_1. The canonical truncation map C P→‖C P‖ 1C_P\to\|C_P\|_1 preserves the fundamental group while eliminating homotopy information above dimension one.

Hence,

π 1(‖C P‖ 1)≅π 1(C P)≅G P, \pi_1(\|C_P\|_1) \cong \pi_1(C_P) \cong G_P,

while π n(‖C P‖ 1)=0\pi_n(\|C_P\|_1)=0 for every n≥2n\geq 2.

Since C PC_P is connected, its 1-truncation is a connected pointed 1-type. By the classification of connected pointed 1-types in homotopy type theory, it is pointed-equivalent to the classifying space of its fundamental group:

‖C P‖ 1≃ *BG P. \|C_P\|_1 \simeq_* BG_P.

Equivalently, the classifying space of a group presented by generators and relations may be obtained by first forming the corresponding homotopy pushout and then applying 1-truncation.

The distinction between C PC_P and ‖C P‖ 1\|C_P\|_1 is essential. The former retains the higher homotopy information of the presentation complex, whereas the latter retains precisely the connected 1-type determined by the presented group.

Formulation in homotopy type theory

In homotopy type theory, the preceding construction can be expressed internally using suspensions, path types, pushout types and homotopy truncation. The generators and relations are encoded by paths and higher-dimensional identifications, rather than by attaching cells to an externally defined topological space.

Throughout this section, XX and RR are regarded as sets (0-types), and P=(X,R,r)P=(X,R,r) denotes the group presentation introduced above.

Suspension as the generator space

Instead of starting with a wedge of circles, consider the suspension of the coproduct of the unit type and the generator set:

S X:=Susp(1+X). S_X := \operatorname{Susp}(\mathbf{1}+X).

The suspension has two distinguished points, north\mathsf{north} and south\mathsf{south}, together with a meridian

merid(a):north=south \mathsf{merid}(a):\mathsf{north}=\mathsf{south}

for each a:1+Xa:\mathbf{1}+X.

The unit summand provides a distinguished reference meridian m *:=merid(inl(*))m_*:=\mathsf{merid}(\mathsf{inl}(*)). Every generator x:Xx:X provides another meridian m x:=merid(inr(x))m_x:=\mathsf{merid}(\mathsf{inr}(x)).

Taking north\mathsf{north} as the basepoint, define the generator loop

γ x:=m x⋅m * −1:north=north. \gamma_x := m_x\cdot m_*^{-1} :\mathsf{north}=\mathsf{north}.

The reference meridian is not an additional group generator. Rather, it allows each generator meridian to be converted into a loop based at north\mathsf{north}.

For a set XX, the resulting pointed type is equivalent to the wedge of circles indexed by XX:

(S X,north)≃ *⋁ x∈XS 1. (S_X,\mathsf{north}) \simeq_* \bigvee_{x\in X}S^1.

Consequently, its fundamental group is the free group on XX.

Generator loops and words

The loop space Ω(S X,north)\Omega(S_X,\mathsf{north}) consists of paths from north\mathsf{north} to itself. Its operations are path concatenation, path inversion and the constant path.

These operations allow words in the generators to be interpreted as loops. In particular, define a map from signed words to based loops by sending a positive generator xx to γ x\gamma_x and a negative generator to γ x −1\gamma_x^{-1}.

For a word w=x 1 ε 1⋯x k ε kw=x_1^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots x_k^{\varepsilon_k}, where each ε i∈{+1,−1}\varepsilon_i\in\{+1,-1\}, its associated loop is

γ w:=γ x 1 ε 1⋅…⋅γ x k ε k. \gamma_w := \gamma_{x_1}^{\varepsilon_1}\cdot \ldots\cdot \gamma_{x_k}^{\varepsilon_k}.

The empty word is interpreted as the constant path. Concatenation of words corresponds to concatenation of loops, and freely reduced words determine the same element of the fundamental group.

More precisely, passing to homotopy classes of loops gives the canonical identification

F(X)≅π 1(S X,north). F(X)\cong\pi_1(S_X,\mathsf{north}).

Thus, the free group is recovered from the fundamental group of the suspension-based generator type.

Relations as higher paths

Each relator a:Ra:R specifies an element r(a):F(X)r(a):F(X), and hence a homotopy class of based loops in S XS_X. Choose a representative loop γ r(a):north=north\gamma_{r(a)}:\mathsf{north}=\mathsf{north}.

The relation r(a)=1r(a)=1 is imposed by requiring this loop to be homotopic to the constant loop. In type-theoretic notation, the corresponding witness has type

γ r(a)=refl north. \gamma_{r(a)}=\mathsf{refl}_{\mathsf{north}}.

Since γ r(a)\gamma_{r(a)} is itself a path, such a witness is a path between paths, or a 2-dimensional path.

The construction does not assume that these witnesses already exist in S XS_X. Instead, they are introduced by the higher inductive construction that attaches the relations.

For each a:Ra:R, the chosen loop determines a pointed map ρ a:S 1→S X\rho_a:S^1\to S_X. Together, these define an attaching map ρ:R×S 1→S X\rho:R\times S^1\to S_X.

The pushout type and 1-truncation

The presentation type is defined by the pushout type of the span

S X←ρR×S 1→pr 1R. S_X \xleftarrow{\rho} R\times S^1 \xrightarrow{\mathrm{pr}_1} R.

Explicitly,

C P HoTT:=Pushout(S X←ρR×S 1→pr 1R). C_P^{\mathrm{HoTT}} := \operatorname{Pushout} \left( S_X \xleftarrow{\rho} R\times S^1 \xrightarrow{\mathrm{pr}_1} R \right).

As a higher inductive type, the pushout has point constructors for the two sides of the span and path constructors identifying their images for each element of R×S 1R\times S^1.

These identifications provide null-homotopies of the relator loops in the resulting type. The pushout therefore implements the relations homotopically, without collapsing the generator space by strict equality.

The resulting type is connected, and its fundamental group is the group specified by the presentation:

π 1(C P HoTT)≅G P. \pi_1(C_P^{\mathrm{HoTT}})\cong G_P.

However, the pushout may retain nontrivial higher homotopy information. Applying 1-truncation produces a connected 1-type whose fundamental group is G PG_P.

Hence, there is a pointed equivalence

‖C P HoTT‖ 1≃ *BG P. \left\|C_P^{\mathrm{HoTT}}\right\|_1 \simeq_* BG_P.

This expresses the classifying space of a presented group entirely in terms of higher inductive types, path operations and truncation. It is the internal homotopy-type-theoretic counterpart of the classical presentation complex.

Motivation from topological quantum gates

One motivation for studying such constructions in homotopy type theory arises from topological quantum computation.

Groups associated with topological configurations, including braid groups, admit descriptions by generators and relations. Their classifying spaces provide homotopy-theoretic models for the corresponding group structures.

In the work of Myers, Sati, and Schreiber on topological quantum gates, homotopy-theoretic methods are used to describe topological structures relevant to quantum gates.

The presentation pushout supplies a general construction of the classifying space of a presented group. The specification of quantum gates additionally requires suitable geometric or representation-theoretic data.
