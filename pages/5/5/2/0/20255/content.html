Idea

In the string theory and M-theory literature, I 8I_8 denotes the degree-eight rational characteristic class

(1)I 8(E)≔148(p 2(E)−14p 1(E) 2)∈H 8(X;ℚ) I_8(E) \;\coloneqq\; \frac{1}{48}\left(p_2(E)-\frac{1}{4}p_1(E)^2\right) \;\in\; H^8(X;\mathbb{Q})

of a real vector bundle E→XE\to X, usually the tangent bundle of spacetime. Here p ip_i are the Pontryagin classes and multiplication denotes the cup product. For a spin bundle, λ(E)≔12p 1(E)\lambda(E)\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{2} p_1(E) is the first fractional Pontryagin class, so that I 8=148(p 2−λ 2)I_8=\tfrac{1}{48}(p_2-\lambda^2). Although λ\lambda is integral on spin bundles, I 8I_8 need not be integral.

The same notation denotes the ordinary closed differential eight-form I 8(R)∈Ω 8(X)I_8(R)\in\Omega^8(X) obtained by Chern-Weil theory from a connection with curvature RR. These are two related objects on ordinary spacetime: a rational characteristic class and its differential-form representative. The characteristic class describes topological constraints, whereas the ordinary form enters local effective actions and equations of motion. The notation X 8X_8 is also common, but its sign and normalization vary. With the usual real-vector trace convention,

I 8(R)=−1192(2π) 4(tr(R 4)−14(tr(R 2)) 2). I_8(R) = -\frac{1}{192}(2\pi)^4 \left( \operatorname{tr}(R^4) - \frac{1}{4} \big(\operatorname{tr}(R^2)\big)^2 \right).

Thus a frequently used convention for X 8X_8 gives X 8=−I 8X_8=-I_8; other authors omit the overall numerical factor. Wedge products are implicit in this curvature formula.

Physically, this polynomial governs the gravitational coupling B 2∧I 8B_2\wedge I_8 in type IIA string theory and its eleven-dimensional counterpart C 3∧I 8C_3\wedge I_8, where C 3C_3 is the supergravity C-field. It contributes to M5-brane anomaly inflow, to the corrected equation for the dual seven-form flux, and to C-field tadpole cancellation in compactifications on eight-manifolds. It is often called the one-loop anomaly term. It should not be identified with the entire M5-brane anomaly polynomial, which also depends on the normal bundle.

Historically, the type IIA one-loop coupling was studied by Vafa–Witten, and its eleven-dimensional interpretation by Duff–Liu–Minasian. Becker–Becker used the correction in compactifications on eight-manifolds. Witten subsequently analyzed both shifted C-field flux quantization and the five-brane effective action. Freed–Harvey–Minasian–Moore established the role of the bulk Chern-Simons interaction in cancellation of the remaining normal-bundle anomaly. Later developments include the integral electric C-field charge, global five-brane anomalies, and inflow computations for wrapped M5-branes.

The original one-loop, flux-quantization and anomaly-inflow articles cited above concern this ordinary (bosonic) form or characteristic class; they do not thereby construct its superspace completion. A separate question is how the curvature coupling extends to a locally supersymmetric interaction, including flux and fermion terms. The superspace approach of Howe–Tsimpis and Souères–Tsimpis instead uses an eight-superform, written ℐ 8(ℛ)\mathcal{I}_8(\mathcal{R}) below, built from the superspace Lorentz curvature ℛ\mathcal{R}. Its components with odd tangent indices enter a deformation problem not present in the ordinary bosonic equation. Its conditional existence results and remaining cohomological checks are discussed below.


Properties

As a higher curvature correction to 11-dimensional supergravity

The ordinary spacetime form and corrected flux equation

The coupling Lagrangian density C 3∧I 8(R)C_3\wedge I_8(R) belongs to the eight-derivative sector of the M-theory effective action, at order ℓ p 6\ell_p^6 (in the 11D Planck constant) relative to the two-derivative action of D=11 supergravity. Its gravitational supersymmetry partners include terms schematically of the form R 4R^4; a full completion also involves the four-form flux and fermions. For the different approaches to these corrections, see at 11D supergravity the section References – Higher Curvature Corrections.

On ordinary eleven-dimensional spacetime, away from magnetic sources, the bosonic anomaly-corrected flux equations are conventionally organized as

(2)dG 4 =0, dG 7 =−12G 4∧G 4+βI 8(R), \begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}G_4 &=0 \,, \\ \mathrm{d}G_7 &= -\frac{1}{2} G_4\wedge G_4 \,+\, \beta \, I_8(R) \mathrlap{\,,} \end{aligned}

through the order under consideration, where the real parameter β\beta is proportional to ℓ p 6\ell_p^6. Its numerical factor and sign depend on conventions for the fluxes and action; the normalization of I 8I_8 here remains that of (1). Reversing the convention for G 7G_7 reverses both terms on the right. The corresponding superspace equation is discussed separately below.

On ordinary spacetime the second equation in (2) packages the corrected C-field equation of motion. The relation between G 7G_7 and ⋆G 4\star G_4 itself receives corrections in β\beta.

The superspace eight-form and Bianchi identities

Let ℳ 11|32\mathcal{M}^{11|32} be the 11D supergravity super spacetime, with Lorentz spin connection and Riemann curvature ℛ\mathcal{R}. Applying the same invariant polynomial defines the closed eight-superform

ℐ 8(ℛ)≔148(p 2(ℛ)−14p 1(ℛ)∧p 1(ℛ)). \mathcal{I}_8(\mathcal{R}) \;\coloneqq\; \frac{1}{48} \Big( p_2(\mathcal{R}) - \tfrac{1}{4} p_1(\mathcal{R}) \wedge p_1(\mathcal{R}) \Big) \mathrlap{\,.}

Here the p i(ℛ)p_i(\mathcal{R}) denote Chern-Weil superforms. The unnormalized X 8X_8 of Souères & Tsimpis, (4.1)–(4.3) is the superform trℛ 4−14(trℛ 2) 2=−192(2π) 4ℐ 8\operatorname{tr}\mathcal{R}^4-\tfrac{1}{4}(\operatorname{tr}\mathcal{R}^2)^2=-192(2\pi)^4\mathcal{I}_8 in the trace convention above. This construction is distinct from specifying the ordinary class I 8(TX)I_8(TX) or ordinary form I 8(R)I_8(R). For an inclusion i:X↪ℳi:X\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M} of the bosonic body, naturality gives

i *ℐ 8(ℛ)=I 8(i *ℛ). i^*\mathcal{I}_8(\mathcal{R}) \;=\; I_8(i^*\mathcal{R}) \mathrlap{\,.}

In the bosonic truncation this recovers the ordinary spacetime curvature expression. But the pullback does not retain all components of the superform: its decomposition ℐ 8=∑ p+q=8(ℐ 8) p,q\mathcal{I}_8=\sum_{p+q=8}(\mathcal{I}_8)_{p,q} also includes components with qq odd tangent indices. The subscript qq counts spinorial indices, not the degree of an ordinary differential form on XX.

Writing the flux superforms as 𝒢 4\mathcal{G}_4 and 𝒢 7\mathcal{G}_7, the proposed superspace extension is

d𝒢 4 =0, d𝒢 7 =−12𝒢 4∧𝒢 4+βℐ 8(ℛ). \begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}\mathcal{G}_4 &= 0 \mathrlap{\,,} \\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{G}_7 &= -\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}_4\wedge\mathcal{G}_4 + \beta\mathcal{I}_8(\mathcal{R}) \mathrlap{\,.} \end{aligned}

These equations must hold in every even/odd component, compatibly with the geometric Bianchi identities and torsion constraints. The existence of the curvature superform ℐ 8\mathcal{I}_8 is not itself the open problem: the issue is finding the constrained supergravity deformation with this source.

For example, treating β\beta as the deformation parameter and writing

𝒢 4 =𝒢 4 (0)+β𝒢 4 (1)+⋯, 𝒢 7 =𝒢 7 (0)+β𝒢 7 (1)+⋯ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{G}_4 &= \mathcal{G}_4^{(0)}+\beta \mathcal{G}_4^{(1)}+\cdots, \\ \mathcal{G}_7 &=\mathcal{G}_7^{(0)} + \beta \mathcal{G}_7^{(1)}+\cdots \end{aligned}

the first-order equations read

d𝒢 4 (1) =0 d𝒢 7 (1) =−𝒢 4 (0)∧𝒢 4 (1)+ℐ 8(ℛ (0)). \begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}\mathcal{G}_4^{(1)} &= 0 \\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{G}_7^{(1)} & = -\mathcal{G}_4^{(0)} \wedge \mathcal{G}_4^{(1)} + \mathcal{I}_8(\mathcal{R}^{(0)}) \mathrlap{\,.} \end{aligned}

Closure of the right-hand side follows from d𝒢 4 (0)=d𝒢 4 (1)=dℐ 8=0\mathrm{d}\mathcal{G}_4^{(0)} = \mathrm{d}\mathcal{G}_4^{(1)} = \mathrm{d}\mathcal{I}_8 = 0.

Superspace deformation theory and the action principle

Howe & Tsimpis 2003 describe deformations using spinorial cohomology, in particular the four-form component with four spinorial indices. They analyze the anomaly-modified seven-form identity and reconstruct low-dimensional superspace data. The geometric analysis of Cederwall, Gran, Nilsson & Tsimpis 2004 solves the torsion Bianchi identities to first order in a general dimension-zero torsion deformation; specifying the deformation sourced by ℐ 8\mathcal{I}_8 remains an additional task.

Souères & Tsimpis 2017, Sec. 4–5 & App. C develop a superform action principle. A closed twelve-superform W 12W_{12} admits an action construction when it is Weil-trivial: besides a Chern-Simons primitive Z 11Z_{11}, it has a gauge-invariant primitive K 11K_{11}, giving the closed Lagrangian superform K 11−Z 11K_{11} - Z_{11}.

For the anomaly correction, a sufficient cohomological condition for the first-order construction is τ\tau-exactness of X 0,8X_{0,8}. This is the purely spinorial component of the curvature eight-superform denoted X 8X_8 in that paper, proportional to (ℐ 8) 0,8(\mathcal{I}_8)_{0,8} in the notation here; it is not a component of the ordinary bosonic form I 8(R)I_8(R). Here τ\tau is the algebraic differential induced by the leading dimension-zero torsion. The authors reduce the possible obstruction to the Spin(1,10)\mathrm{Spin}(1,10) representations (04000)(04000), (03002)(03002) and (02004)(02004), and verify cancellation of the first and third. The (03002)(03002) cancellation is left unchecked. The verified cancellations depend on the relative coefficient −14-\tfrac{1}{4} in this curvature superform.

Their classification assumes perturbative solvability of the superspace Bianchi identities; their uniqueness statement assumes an invariant starting at fourth order in fields. Integrability to higher orders has further conditions. Consequently these results do not constitute an unconditional existence proof for the complete anomaly-deformed superspace system.

The distinction is between a first-order action invariant, a solution of all constrained superspace Bianchi identities, and extension to higher perturbative orders. The cited superspace work supports the expected completion but does not settle all three questions. Results in selected component sectors or scattering amplitudes do not by themselves fill this gap; see the references on the superspace deformation problem and the surrounding bibliography.

The possible order-ℓ p 3\ell_p^3 deformation

The order-ℓ p 3\ell_p^3 class studied by Tsimpis 2004 requires a separate qualification. Its relation to shifting G 4G_4 by a multiple of ℓ p 3p 1(R)\ell_p^3p_1(R) makes its global interpretation sensitive to C-field flux quantization. On backgrounds with trivial first Pontryagin class it can be removed by a C-field redefinition; in the nontrivial case removing it changes the flux quantization condition. Its existence as a possible supersymmetric deformation does not fix a mandatory nonzero coefficient in the quantum M-theory action.

In particular, one cannot obtain a general corrected equation merely by appending a mixed ℓ p 3G 4∧λ(R)\ell_p^3G_4\wedge\lambda(R) term to (2). Algebraically, a change of variables G˜ 4=G 4+aℓ p 3λ(R)\widetilde G_4=G_4+a\ell_p^3\lambda(R) gives

−12G 4∧G 4=−12G˜ 4∧G˜ 4+aℓ p 3G˜ 4∧λ(R)−a 2ℓ p 62λ(R)∧λ(R). -\frac{1}{2}G_4\wedge G_4 \;=\; -\frac{1}{2}\widetilde G_4\wedge\widetilde G_4 + a\ell_p^3\widetilde G_4\wedge\lambda(R) - \frac{a^2\ell_p^6}{2}\lambda(R)\wedge\lambda(R) \mathrlap{\,.}

Thus even this change of flux variable produces an additional order-ℓ p 6\ell_p^6 curvature term. The coefficient, global flux convention and any further corrections have to be specified together.

Inflow to M5-brane anomalies

Consider an 11-dimensional spin-manifold X 11X^{11} and a 2-parameter family of 6-dimensional submanifolds Q M5↪X 11Q_{M5}\hookrightarrow X^{11}. When regarded as a family of worldvolumes of an M5-brane, the family of normal bundles N XQ M5N_X Q_{M5} of this inclusion carries a characteristic class

(3)I M5≔I ψ M5+I C∈H 8(F×Q M5,ℤ), I^{M5} \;\coloneqq\; I^{M5}_{\psi} + I_C \;\in\; H^8(F\times Q_{M5},\mathbb{Z}) \mathrlap{\,,}

where

  1. the first summand is the class of the chiral anomaly of chiral fermions on Q M5Q_{M5} (Witten 96, (5.1)),

  2. the second term is the class of the quantum anomaly of a self-dual higher gauge field (Witten 96, (5.4)).

Moreover, there is the restriction of the I 8I_8-term (1) to Q M5Q_{M5}, hence to the tangent bundle of X 11X^{11} restricted to Q M5Q_{M5} (the “anomaly inflow” from the bulk spacetime to the M5-brane)

(4)I 8| M5≔I 8(T Q M5X)∈H 8(F×Q M5,ℤ). I_8\vert_{M5} \;\coloneqq\; I_8\big(T_{Q_{M5}}X\big) \;\in\; H^8(F\times Q_{M5},\mathbb{Z}).

The sum of these cohomology classes, evaluated on the fundamental class of Q M5Q_{M5}, is proportional to the second Pontryagin class of the normal bundle

(5)I M5+I 8| M5=124p 2(N Q M5) I^{M5}+I_8\vert_{M5} =\frac{1}{24}p_2(N_{Q_{M5}})

(Witten 96, (5.7)).

This result used to be “somewhat puzzling” (Witten 96, p. 35) since consistency of the M5-brane in M-theory should require its total quantum anomaly to vanish. But p 2(N Q M5)p_2(N_{Q_{M5}}) does not in general vanish, and the right conditions to require under which it does vanish were “not clear” (Witten 96, p. 37).

(For more details on the computations involved in this and the following arguments, see also Bilal–Metzger 03.)

A resolution was proposed in Freed–Harvey–Minasian–Moore 98, see also Bah–Bonetti–Minasian–Nardoni 18, (5), BBMN 19, (2.9) and Appendices A.4, A.5. By this proposal, the anomaly inflow from the bulk would not be just I 8I_8, as in (4), but would be all of the following fiber integration

(6)π *(−16G 4∧G 4∧G 4+G 4∧I 8)=−124p 2+12(G 4 M5) 2+I 8. \pi_*\left(-\frac{1}{6}G_4\wedge G_4\wedge G_4+G_4\wedge I_8\right) = -\frac{1}{24}p_2+\frac{1}{2}(G^{M5}_4)^2+I_8.

Here we used this Prop. to find that

π *(χ 3)=2p 2 \pi_*(\chi^3)=2p_2

which would cancel against the first term 124p 2\tfrac{1}{24}p_2 in (6). Hence with this proposal, the remaining M5-brane anomaly (5) would be canceled – except for a remaining term 12(G 4 M5) 2\tfrac{1}{2}(G^{M5}_4)^2 which is ignored by fiat.
