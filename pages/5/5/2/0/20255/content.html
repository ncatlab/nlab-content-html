Idea

The class

In the string theory and M-theory literature, I 8I_8 denotes the degree-eight rational characteristic class

(1)I 8(E)≔148(p 2(E)−14p 1(E) 2)∈H 8(X;ℚ) I_8(E) \;\coloneqq\; \frac{1}{48}\left( p_2(E) - \frac{1}{4}p_1(E)^2 \right) \;\in\; H^8(X;\mathbb{Q})

of a real vector bundle E→XE\to X, usually the tangent bundle E∶−TX 1,10E \coloneq T X^{1,10} of an 11-dimensional spacetime X 1,10X^{1,10}. Here p ip_i denote the Pontryagin classes and multiplication denotes their cup product. For EE spin structured, λ(E)≔12p 1(E)\lambda(E) \coloneqq \tfrac{1}{2} p_1(E) is the first fractional Pontryagin class, so that I 8=148(p 2−λ 2)I_8=\tfrac{1}{48}(p_2-\lambda^2). Although λ\lambda is integral on spin bundles, I 8I_8 need not be integral.

When EE is equipped with a connection ∇\nabla with curvature R=R(∇)R = R(\nabla) the same notation is used for the corresponding curvature characteristic form, the closed differential 8-form I 8(R)∈Ω dR 8(X)I_8(R) \in \Omega^8_{dR}(X).

Sometimes ±I 8\pm I_8 is denoted “X 8X_8” and generally sign and normalization conventions vary. With the usual real-vector trace convention, we have

(2)I 8(R)=−11921(2π) 4(tr(R 4)−14(tr(R 2)) 2), I_8(R) = -\frac{1}{192}\,\frac{1}{(2\pi)^4} \left( \operatorname{tr}(R^4) - \frac{1}{4} \big(\operatorname{tr}(R^2)\big)^2 \right) \mathrlap{\,,}

where multiplication now denotes wedge product of differential forms.

By Chern-Weil theory, the de Rham class of this form is the image of I 8(E)I_8(E) under extension of scalars from ℚ\mathbb{Q} to ℝ\mathbb{R}:

Ω cl 8(X) ⟶ H dR 8(X) ≃ H 8(X;ℝ) ⟵ H 8(X;ℚ) I 8(R) longmapsto [I 8(R)] dR longleftrightarrow I 8(E)⊗1 ⟵ I 8(E). \begin{array}{ccccccc} \Omega^8_{\mathrm{cl}}(X) &\longrightarrow& H^8_{dR}(X) &\simeq& H^8(X;\mathbb{R}) &\longleftarrow& H^8(X;\mathbb{Q}) \\ I_8(R)&\longmapsto&[I_8(R)]_{dR} &\longleftrightarrow& I_8(E)\otimes 1 &\longleftarrow& I_8(E) \mathrlap{\,.} \end{array}

Here Ω cl 8(X)\Omega^8_{\mathrm{cl}}(X) denotes the closed 8-forms on the base XX. Changing the connection changes the representative by an exact form, not the characteristic class.

As a gravitational coupling

In string theory/M-theory, I 8I_8 appears in a leading higher curvature correction coupling the higher gauge fields to gravity:

  1. The one-loop effective action functional of type IIA string theory contains, in addition to that of plain type IIA supergravity, a term proportional to

    (3)∫ X 1,9B 2∧I 8(R) \int_{X^{1,9}} B_2 \wedge I_8(R)

    for B 2B_2 the 2-form gauge potential of the B-field.

    For this reason I 8I_8 is widely known also as the one-loop term, following Vafa & Witten 1995, Duff, Liu, Minasian 1995.

  2. The leading eight-derivative correction to the action functional of 11D supergravity contains a term proportional to

    (4)∫ X 1,10C 3∧I 8(R), \int_{X^{1,10}} C_3 \wedge I_8(R) \mathrlap{\,,}

    where C 3C_3 is the 3-form gauge potential of the supergravity C-field. This is one term in a supersymmetric interaction, not the entire correction.

These two terms (3) and (4) are related under duality between M-theory and type IIA string theory.

(Here in both cases R=R(∇)R = R(\nabla) is the Riemann curvature of the respective Levi-Civita spin connection reflecting the field of gravity.)

Under passage to Euler-Lagrange equations of motion, the term (4) modifies the equation of motion of the supergravity C-field. Written as a Bianchi identity for the dual flux density G 7G_7 (which equals ⋆G 4\star G_4 to leading order), the modified equation reads

(5)dG 7=−12G 4∧G 4+βI 8(R) \mathrm{d} G_7 = -\tfrac{1}{2} G_4 \wedge G_4 + \beta I_8(R)

for some real coefficient β\beta, carrying physical unit ℓ P 6\ell_P^6, for ℓ P\ell_P the Planck length in 11D (sign conventions as below). Thereby I 8I_8 appears then also in the C-field tadpole cancellation for KK-compactifications on 8-manifolds.

Original discussions

Historically, the type IIA one-loop coupling was studied by Vafa & Witten 1995, and its eleven-dimensional interpretation by Duff, Liu & Minasian 1995. Becker & Becker 1996 used the correction in compactifications on eight-manifolds. Witten 1996 subsequently analyzed both shifted C-field flux quantization and the five-brane effective action. Freed, Harvey, Minasian & Moore 1998 established the role of the bulk Chern-Simons interaction in cancellation of the remaining normal-bundle anomaly. Later developments include the formulation of C-field flux quantization by Diaconescu, Freed & Moore 2003, and anomaly inflow computations for wrapped M5-branes by BBMN 2019.

Superspace refinement

The characteristic-class and anomaly arguments above identify the ordinary curvature coupling. They do not by themselves determine

  1. how the curvature coupling extends to a locally supersymmetric interaction, including flux and fermion (gravitino) terms,

  2. how implementation of the modified equation of motion (5) on super-spacetime (“superspace”) affects the torsion constraints of 11D supergravity.

The superspace approach of Howe & Tsimpis 2003 and Souères & Tsimpis 2017 uses a superform enhancement of I 8(R)I_8(R) (2), denoted ℐ 8(ℛ)\mathcal{I}_8(\mathcal{R}) below, built from the superspace Lorentz curvature ℛ\mathcal{R}, as a source in the Bianchi identity of the super flux 7-form.

In ordinary 11D supergravity the flux superforms have at most two spinorial indices. The curvature superform ℐ 8\mathcal{I}_8, however, generally has components with as many as eight. Thus the sourced Bianchi identity cannot in general be satisfied while retaining the classical flux ansatz. In the deformation framework of Howe & Tsimpis 2003, a nontrivial first-order change of the local supergravity equations is encoded by the previously vanishing component G αβγδG_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} of the 4-form, modulo field redefinitions; the 4-form Bianchi identities then relate this component to a change of the dimension-zero torsion.

Three questions must be distinguished: constructing a first-order supersymmetric action term; determining the corresponding deformation of the constrained superspace geometry; and extending that deformation consistently to higher orders. The cited superspace literature gives a cohomological framework for these questions and substantial partial results. In particular, Souères & Tsimpis 2017 identify a sufficient condition for the first-order anomaly superinvariant and verify part of it. Their remaining check, and the further questions concerning the full deformation, are described below.


Properties

As a higher curvature correction to 11-dimensional supergravity

The ordinary spacetime form and corrected flux equation

The coupling Lagrangian density C 3∧I 8(R)C_3\wedge I_8(R) (4) belongs to the eight-derivative sector of the M-theory effective action, at order ℓ p 6\ell_p^6 (with ℓ p\ell_p the 11D Planck length) relative to the two-derivative action of D=11 supergravity. Its gravitational supersymmetry partners include terms schematically of the form R 4R^4; a full completion also involves the supergravity C-field flux density G 4G_4 and the gravitino. For the different approaches to these corrections, see at 11D supergravity the section References – Higher Curvature Corrections.

On ordinary eleven-dimensional spacetime, away from magnetic sources, the bosonic anomaly-corrected flux equations are conventionally organized as

(6)dG 4 =0, dG 7 =−12G 4∧G 4+βI 8(R), \begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}G_4 &=0 \,, \\ \mathrm{d}G_7 &= -\frac{1}{2} G_4\wedge G_4 \,+\, \beta \, I_8(R) \mathrlap{\,,} \end{aligned}

through the order under consideration, where the real parameter β\beta is proportional to ℓ p 6\ell_p^6. Its numerical factor and sign depend on conventions for the fluxes and action; the normalization of I 8I_8 here remains that of (1). Reversing the convention for G 7G_7 reverses both terms on the right. For example:

  • Souères & Tsimpis 2017, (4.2)–(4.3) write dG 7+12G 4∧G 4=ℓ 6X 8\mathrm{d}G_7 + \tfrac{1}{2}G_4\wedge G_4 = \ell^6 X_8 with X 8≔trR 4−14(trR 2) 2X_8 \coloneqq \operatorname{tr}R^4 - \tfrac{1}{4}(\operatorname{tr}R^2)^2 and a numerical factor absorbed into ℓ\ell. Since X 8=−192(2π) 4I 8X_8 = -192 (2\pi)^4 I_8 (by eq:TheChernWeilForm), this is (6) with β=−192(2π) 4ℓ 6\beta = -192(2\pi)^4\ell^6 in their units.

  • Howe & Tsimpis 2003, (56) write dG 7=+12G 4∧G 4+βX 8\mathrm{d}G_7 = +\tfrac{1}{2}G_4\wedge G_4 + \beta X_8, with the opposite sign convention for G 7G_7; their parameter β\beta must also be translated when comparing the source coefficients.

On ordinary spacetime the second equation in (6) packages the corrected C-field equation of motion. To leading order G 7=⋆G 4G_7=\star G_4, but this relation itself receives corrections at order β\beta. Concretely (Souères & Tsimpis 2017, (4.10)–(4.11)): if the bosonic corrected action is ∫(R⋆1−12G 4∧⋆G 4−16C 3∧G 4∧G 4+ℓ 6X 8∧C 3+ΔL⋆1)\int \big(R\star 1 - \tfrac{1}{2}G_4\wedge\star G_4 - \tfrac{1}{6}C_3\wedge G_4\wedge G_4 + \ell^6 X_8\wedge C_3 + \Delta L\star 1\big) with ΔL\Delta L gauge invariant, then the variation of ΔL\Delta L with respect to C 3C_3 is exact, and is absorbed into a corrected duality relation G 7=⋆G 4+𝒪(ℓ 6)G_7 = \star G_4 + \mathcal{O}(\ell^6).

This bosonic flux equation displays only part of the correction. The curvature coupling and its supersymmetry partners also modify the gravitational and fermionic equations. On superspace these changes must be compatible with all components of the flux and geometric Bianchi identities, as discussed below.

Undeformed supergravity on superspace

Let ℳ 11|32\mathcal{M}^{11|32} be the 11-dimensional super spacetime, with super-coframe (E a,E α)(E^a, E^\alpha) (a=0,⋯,10a = 0, \cdots, 10 a vector index, α=1,⋯,32\alpha = 1, \cdots, 32 a Majorana spinor index), equipped with a Lorentz-valued spin connection with torsion T AT^A and curvature ℛ\mathcal{R}. Superforms decompose by the number pp of even and qq of odd form indices: Ω n=⨁ p+q=nΩ p,q\Omega^n = \bigoplus_{p+q = n}\Omega^{p,q}. Assigning engineering dimension −1-1 to E aE^a and −1/2-1/2 to E αE^\alpha, the components of a given superform have dimension increasing by 1/21/2 with each odd index traded for an even one.

The torsion constraint. The superspace formulation of ordinary 11D supergravity goes back to Cremmer & Ferrara 1980 and Brink & Howe 1980. Central to it is the constraint on the dimension-zero torsion

(7)T αβ c=−i(γ c) αβ. T_{\alpha\beta}{}^c \;=\; - \mathrm{i}\, (\gamma^c)_{\alpha\beta} \,.

Sign and normalization vary between references, e.g. Souères & Tsimpis 2017, (2.4), CGNT 2004, (2.9). The dimension-zero equation should be distinguished from a full set of conventional torsion constraints, which also specifies components with vector indices.

The geometric formulation. Howe 1997 derives the on-shell theory from the standard dimension-zero torsion using Weyl superspace, with structure group Spin(1,10)×ℝ +\mathrm{Spin}(1,10)\times\mathbb{R}^{+}. The scale curvature vanishes, so the scale connection can be removed locally. Passing to the usual formulation globally requires attention to its holonomy; simple connectivity is a sufficient hypothesis used in CGNT 2004, §3. It is not a prerequisite for the local argument. See also Howe & Tsimpis 2003, §2 and CGNT 2004, §2.5–2.6.

The duality-symmetric flux formulation. In Giotopoulos, Sati & Schreiber 2024, Def. 2.74, the super-coframe (e a,ψ α)(e^a,\psi^\alpha) and spin connection ω\omega obey the full bosonic super-torsion constraint T a=de a+ω a b∧e b−ψ¯Γ aψ=0T^a=\mathrm{d}e^a+\omega^a{}_b\wedge e^b-\overline{\psi}\Gamma^a\psi=0. This includes the dimension-zero constraint in their conventions, together with vanishing vector-valued torsion components of higher dimension.

A detailed equivalence theorem, with computer algebra checks, is Giotopoulos, Sati & Schreiber 2024, Thm. 3.1. Consider an (11|32)(11\vert 32)-dimensional super-spacetime satisfying the above bosonic torsion constraint, with no constraint imposed on the gravitino field strength. It carries super flux densities of the form (GSS24, (8)–(9))

(8)G 4 s =14!(G 4) a 1⋯a 4e a 1⋯e a 4+12(ψ¯Γ a 1a 2ψ)e a 1e a 2, G 7 s =17!(G 7) a 1⋯a 7e a 1⋯e a 7+15!(ψ¯Γ a 1⋯a 5ψ)e a 1⋯e a 5,dG 4 s =0, dG 7 s =12G 4 s∧G 4 s, \begin{aligned} G^s_4 &= \tfrac{1}{4!}(G_4)_{a_1 \cdots a_4}\, e^{a_1}\cdots e^{a_4} + \tfrac{1}{2}\big(\overline{\psi}\,\Gamma_{a_1 a_2}\psi\big)\, e^{a_1} e^{a_2} \,, \\ G^s_7 &= \tfrac{1}{7!}(G_7)_{a_1 \cdots a_7}\, e^{a_1}\cdots e^{a_7} + \tfrac{1}{5!}\big(\overline{\psi}\,\Gamma_{a_1 \cdots a_5}\psi\big)\, e^{a_1} \cdots e^{a_5} \,, \end{aligned} \qquad \begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}G^s_4 &= 0 \,, \\ \mathrm{d}G^s_7 &= \tfrac{1}{2} G^s_4 \wedge G^s_4 \,, \end{aligned}

if and only if the underlying fields solve the equations of motion of 11D supergravity (the C-field, Rarita-Schwinger and Einstein equations, with G 7=⋆G 4G_7 = \star G_4 implied), and the superfields are the unique rheonomic extension of their restriction to the bosonic body. (The sign convention for G 7G_7 here is opposite to that of (6).) In this formulation:

  • The vanishing of the (ψ¯κψ)(\overline{\psi}\kappa\psi)-component of the gravitino field strength follows from imposing both flux Bianchi identities, instead of being obtained through a scaling condition. These supply the needed 2-index and 5-index constraints (GSS24, Rem. 3.10).

  • Both directions of the equivalence are proven. The converse direction, that the Bianchi identities imply no constraints beyond the equations of motion, makes up the bulk of the work (GSS24, footnote 13 and Conclusion).

These are different choices of hypotheses and variables for the undeformed theory. The duality-symmetric theorem does not by itself settle the analogous equivalence for a deformed theory. Earlier indications of this formulation are in Castellani, D’Auria & Fré 1991, §III.8.5 and Candiello & Lechner 1994, §6; cf. Howe & Tsimpis 2003, §5.

This statement is about constrained supergravity, not an arbitrary closed 4-form on a supermanifold.

The superspace eight-form

Applying the same invariant polynomial as in (1) to the superspace curvature ℛ\mathcal{R} defines the closed eight-superform

(10)ℐ 8(ℛ)≔148(p 2(ℛ)−14p 1(ℛ)∧p 1(ℛ)). \mathcal{I}_8(\mathcal{R}) \;\coloneqq\; \frac{1}{48} \Big( p_2(\mathcal{R}) - \tfrac{1}{4} p_1(\mathcal{R}) \wedge p_1(\mathcal{R}) \Big) \mathrlap{\,.}

Here the p i(ℛ)p_i(\mathcal{R}) denote Chern-Weil superforms. The unnormalized X 8X_8 of Howe & Tsimpis 2003, (56) and Souères & Tsimpis 2017, (4.2) is the superform trℛ 4−14(trℛ 2) 2=−192(2π) 4ℐ 8\operatorname{tr}\mathcal{R}^4-\tfrac{1}{4}(\operatorname{tr}\mathcal{R}^2)^2=-192(2\pi)^4\mathcal{I}_8 in the trace convention (2) above. This construction is distinct from specifying the ordinary class I 8(TX)I_8(T X) or ordinary form I 8(R)I_8(R).

Pullback to the bosonic body. For an inclusion i:X↪ℳi \colon X \hookrightarrow \mathcal{M} of the bosonic body, naturality gives

The sourced Bianchi identities. Writing the flux superforms as 𝒢 4\mathcal{G}_4 and 𝒢 7\mathcal{G}_7, the proposed superspace extension of (6) is

The existence of the curvature superform ℐ 8\mathcal{I}_8 is not itself the open problem: the issue is finding the constrained supergravity deformation with this source.

Treating β\beta as a formal deformation parameter and writing

There is also a distinction between expanding differential forms and expanding their components in a moving super-coframe. If E A=E (0)A+βE (1)A+⋯E^A=E^{(0)A}+\beta E^{(1)A}+\cdots, the latter expansion includes coframe corrections, and the component expressions for the exterior derivative change with the geometry. The displayed form equations use the fixed exterior derivative d\mathrm{d}; when converting them into frame components the coframe terms must be included.

Effect of the source term on the superspace geometry

For flux superforms of the undeformed form (8), with components of at most two spinorial indices, the combination d𝒢 7+12𝒢 4∧𝒢 4\mathrm{d}\mathcal{G}_7 + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}_4\wedge\mathcal{G}_4 has components only with at most four spinorial indices. This holds whatever the torsion, since τ\tau raises the number of spinorial indices by two. On-shell, all of these components vanish (above).

The source can have components with five or more spinorial indices, including the generically nonzero (ℐ 8) 0,8(\mathcal{I}_8)_{0,8}. Thus the classical flux ansatz cannot solve the sourced identity for general fields. This is a statement about a universal deformation of the theory; some source components may vanish on special backgrounds, for example (ℐ 8) 0,8(\mathcal{I}_8)_{0,8} when G abcd=0G_{abcd}=0.

The lowest sourced equation requires new components of 𝒢 7\mathcal{G}_7. Identifying a nontrivial change of 𝒢 4\mathcal{G}_4 and torsion requires the subsequent equations and the deformation cohomology; it does not follow from counting spinorial indices alone. The expectation that the full I 8I_8 correction changes the torsion constraint is recorded in Giotopoulos, Sati & Schreiber 2024, §2.1.7, following CGNT 2004. The superinvariant via the action principle

Souères & Tsimpis 2017 (see also Howe & Tsimpis 2003, §5, Souères 2018) approach the corresponding first-order superinvariant through the superspace action principle (“ectoplasm”): a closed 11-superform L 11L_{11} gives an invariant by integration over the bosonic body. When the form is constructed using the classical on-shell constraints, invariance is understood modulo the classical equations of motion. This is the setting for a first-order action correction, not an off-shell or all-order construction. For a Chern-Simons term Z 11Z_{11} with closed 12-superform W 12=dZ 11W_{12} = \mathrm{d}Z_{11}, this applies if W 12W_{12} is Weil-trivial: if it also has a gauge-invariant primitive K 11K_{11} built locally and tensorially from the constrained on-shell superfields. Then L 11≔Z 11−K 11L_{11} \coloneqq Z_{11} - K_{11} is closed. Weil-triviality does not follow from the triviality of the de Rham cohomology of superspace, since it refers to the constrained (on-shell) superfields (Souères & Tsimpis 2017, §2.1).

Moreover, a formal extension in powers of the coefficient β\beta tests the integrability of this particular deformation. It should not be identified with the entire M-theory derivative expansion, which may contain independent higher-derivative interactions.

What is known and what is open

The status of the local constrained superspace construction in the cited literature can be summarized as follows.

  • The undeformed theory. Its geometric and superform formulations are established, with their respective conventional constraints and local/global qualifications (Howe 1997, Giotopoulos, Sati & Schreiber 2024). The latter gives a detailed equivalence theorem for the duality-symmetric flux formulation.

  • The deformation framework. The first-order 4-form deformation is encoded by [G 0,4 (1)]∈H s 0,4(phys)[G^{(1)}_{0,4}]\in H^{0,4}_s(\mathrm{phys}), with an explicit low-dimensional reconstruction (Howe & Tsimpis 2003). The geometric approach determines torsion in terms of the deformation tensors X,YX,Y and explains how to obtain corrected dynamics from them (CGNT 2004). Neither parametrization alone supplies the required local expressions for the I 8I_8 source.

  • A sufficient condition for the first-order invariant. τ\tau-exactness of X 0,8X_{0,8} implies Weil-triviality of G 4 (0)∧X 8G^{(0)}_4\wedge X_8, hence an on-shell first-order superinvariant. The high-vector-degree τ\tau-cohomology vanishing used in this construction has been proved. Souères & Tsimpis 2017 verify the (04000)(04000) and (02004)(02004) cancellations, and explicitly leave the (03002)(03002) calculation unresolved.

The remaining tasks in this programme are consequently distinct:

  1. Complete the cited τ\tau-exactness calculation (or give another proof of the required Weil-triviality). The unperformed (03002)(03002) check is a gap in that particular construction; it is not evidence that the anomaly interaction fails to have a supersymmetric completion.

  2. Construct the I 8I_8-sourced deformation as a local solution of the coupled flux and geometric Bianchi identities, including an explicit representative for G 0,4 (1)G^{(1)}_{0,4} and the resulting torsion and field equations. The action-principle argument for an invariant and the reconstruction of this full constrained geometry must not be conflated. The general comparison of geometric and 4-form/7-form descriptions also requires the qualifications discussed in CGNT 2004, §2.7.

  3. Establish higher-order integrability, beginning with the inhomogeneous second-order problem (17). Neither the first-order reconstruction nor the high-vector-degree τ\tau-cohomology vanishing alone proves this. Uniqueness claims must retain the restrictions on field content, order and allowed redefinitions under which they were made.

The strong existence statement in the abstract of Howe & Tsimpis 2003 should be read together with the consistency premise and indirect argument in its §5, and the more explicit sufficient-condition analysis of Souères & Tsimpis 2017, §4.2 & §5. The statements above describe what those constructions supply and leave to be supplied, rather than asserting the absence of every possible alternative existence argument.

There is also substantial progress in component sectors and amplitudes. For example, Peeters, Vanhove & Westerberg 2000 analyze the anomaly-related R 4R^4 sector before including gauge-field terms, while Hyakutake 2025 studies (DF) 4(DF)^4 terms and associated fermion bilinears. Such results constrain the completion, but their stated scope is narrower than a solution of all the sourced superspace identities. See also higher curvature corrections to D=11 supergravity – references.

Finally, local superspace consistency and global C-field flux quantization are different questions. A local solution must still be related to globally defined, quantized fields; ordinary characteristic-class and anomaly arguments do not by themselves solve the local superspace deformation problem, and local Bianchi calculations do not by themselves settle its global refinement.

The possible order-ℓ p 3\ell_p^3 deformation

Tsimpis 2004 computes H s 0,4(phys)H^{0,4}_s(\mathrm{phys}) up to order ℓ 3\ell^3:

  • At orders ℓ\ell and ℓ 2\ell^2 it vanishes, so there are no deformations at these orders within this local field-strength framework.

  • At order ℓ 3\ell^3 it is one-dimensional, generated by a combination of the two G 2G^2-terms in the AA- and BB-components of (14) (Tsimpis 2004, (4.8)–(4.10)).

The generator is absorbed by the shift of the super 4-form 𝒢 4↦𝒢 4−β′ℓ 34πtrℛ 2\mathcal{G}_4 \mapsto \mathcal{G}_4 - \tfrac{\beta' \ell^3}{4\pi}\operatorname{tr}\mathcal{R}^2, which is locally a shift of the super 3-form potential by a Chern-Simons form (Tsimpis 2004, (4.11)–(4.15)). Here β′\beta' is an arbitrary real parameter, unrelated to β\beta above. The potential redefinition uses the connection through a Lorentz Chern-Simons form; it is not a redefinition by a gauge-invariant tensorial 3-form. Its local availability therefore does not contradict the nontriviality of the class with physical tensorial coefficients.

Inflow to M5-brane anomalies

Consider an 11-dimensional spin-manifold X 11X^{11} and a 2-parameter family of 6-dimensional submanifolds Q M5↪X 11Q_{M5}\hookrightarrow X^{11}. When regarded as a family of worldvolumes of an M5-brane, the family of normal bundles N XQ M5N_X Q_{M5} of this inclusion carries a characteristic class

(18)I M5≔I ψ M5+I C∈H 8(F×Q M5,ℤ), I^{M5} \;\coloneqq\; I^{M5}_{\psi} + I_C \;\in\; H^8(F\times Q_{M5},\mathbb{Z}) \mathrlap{\,,}

where

  1. the first summand is the class of the chiral anomaly of chiral fermions on Q M5Q_{M5} (Witten 96, (5.1)),

  2. the second term is the class of the quantum anomaly of a self-dual higher gauge field (Witten 96, (5.4)).

Moreover, there is the restriction of the I 8I_8-term (1) to Q M5Q_{M5}, hence to the tangent bundle of X 11X^{11} restricted to Q M5Q_{M5} (the “anomaly inflow” from the bulk spacetime to the M5-brane)

(19)I 8| M5≔I 8(T Q M5X)∈H 8(F×Q M5,ℤ). I_8\vert_{M5} \;\coloneqq\; I_8\big(T_{Q_{M5}}X\big) \;\in\; H^8(F\times Q_{M5},\mathbb{Z}).

The sum of these cohomology classes, evaluated on the fundamental class of Q M5Q_{M5}, is proportional to the second Pontryagin class of the normal bundle

(20)I M5+I 8| M5=124p 2(N Q M5) I^{M5}+I_8\vert_{M5} =\frac{1}{24}p_2(N_{Q_{M5}})

(Witten 96, (5.7)).

This result used to be “somewhat puzzling” (Witten 96, p. 35) since consistency of the M5-brane in M-theory should require its total quantum anomaly to vanish. But p 2(N Q M5)p_2(N_{Q_{M5}}) does not in general vanish, and the right conditions to require under which it does vanish were “not clear” (Witten 96, p. 37).

(For more details on the computations involved in this and the following arguments, see also Bilal–Metzger 03.)

A resolution was proposed in Freed–Harvey–Minasian–Moore 98, see also Bah–Bonetti–Minasian–Nardoni 18, (5), BBMN 19, (2.9) and Appendices A.4, A.5. By this proposal, the anomaly inflow from the bulk would not be just I 8I_8, as in (19), but would be all of the following fiber integration

(21)π *(−16G 4∧G 4∧G 4+G 4∧I 8)=−124p 2+12(G 4 M5) 2+I 8. \pi_*\left(-\frac{1}{6}G_4\wedge G_4\wedge G_4+G_4\wedge I_8\right) = -\frac{1}{24}p_2+\frac{1}{2}(G^{M5}_4)^2+I_8.

Here we used this Prop. to find that

π *(χ 3)=2p 2 \pi_*(\chi^3)=2p_2

which would cancel against the first term 124p 2\tfrac{1}{24}p_2 in (21). Hence with this proposal, the remaining M5-brane anomaly (20) would be canceled – except for a remaining term 12(G 4 M5) 2\tfrac{1}{2}(G^{M5}_4)^2 which is ignored by fiat.
