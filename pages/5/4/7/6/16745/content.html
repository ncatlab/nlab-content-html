Idea

The concept of Borcherds algebras is a generalization of that of Kac-Moody algebras (hence also called generalized Kac-Moody algebras or Borcherds-Kac-Moody algebras, “BKM algebras”) obtained by allowing imaginary simple roots.

Recall that a (symmetrizable) Kac-Moody algebra is presented by generators and relations as being glued from copies of 𝔰𝔩 ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2) , one triple (e i,h i,f i)(e_i, h_i, f_i) for each simple root α i\alpha_i, according to a Cartan matrix (a ij)(a_{i j}) whose diagonal entries are all a ii=2a_{i i} = 2 and whose off-diagonal entries are non-positive integers, subject to the Serre relations. That all diagonal entries are positive means that all simple roots have positive norm (“real roots”). This makes the adjoint action of each of these 𝔰𝔩 ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2) -triples locally finite (“integrable”) and yields a Weyl group generated by the reflections in all the simple roots.

The idea of Borcherds 1988 is to keep this kind of presentation but to admit also diagonal entries a ii≤0a_{i i} \leq 0, hence simple roots α i\alpha_i of non-positive norm (“imaginary simple roots”). For these:

  • the triple (e i,h i,f i)(e_i, h_i, f_i) still spans a copy of 𝔰𝔩 ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2) if a ii<0a_{i i} \lt 0, but a copy of the 3-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra if a ii=0a_{i i} = 0;

  • there are no Serre relations of the form (ad e i) n(e j)=0(\mathrm{ad}_{e_i})^n(e_j) = 0 (so that e ie_i need not act locally nilpotently), only the relation [e i,e j]=0=[f i,f j][e_i, e_j] = 0 = [f_i, f_j] whenever a ij=0a_{i j} = 0;

  • there is no corresponding reflection: the Weyl group is generated by the reflections in the real simple roots only.

The effect is that Borcherds algebras behave like Kac-Moody algebras with some “freely generated directions” adjoined:

For example, if no two imaginary simple roots are orthogonal, then a Borcherds algebra decomposes as a direct sum 𝔲 −⊕(𝔤 J+𝔥)⊕𝔲 +\mathfrak{u}^- \oplus (\mathfrak{g}_J + \mathfrak{h}) \oplus \mathfrak{u}^+, where 𝔤 J\mathfrak{g}_J is the Kac-Moody algebra of the real simple roots, 𝔥\mathfrak{h} is the Cartan subalgebra, and 𝔲 ±\mathfrak{u}^\pm are free Lie algebras (Jurisich 1998).

Nevertheless, most of the structure theory of Kac-Moody algebras carries over (Borcherds 1988, Kac 1990, §11.13), notably the invariant bilinear form, the root space decomposition with finite-dimensional root spaces, the Weyl group, and a version of the Weyl-Kac character formula and its denominator identity, now with an extra correction term contributed by the imaginary simple roots.

More conceptually, Borcherds algebras are essentially characterized as the Lie algebras carrying an almost positive definite contravariant form (Borcherds 1988, §3, Borcherds 1995a): If 𝔤=⨁ n∈ℤ𝔤 n\mathfrak{g} = \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} \mathfrak{g}_n is a ℤ\mathbb{Z}-graded Lie algebra with 𝔤 n\mathfrak{g}_n finite-dimensional for n≠0n \neq 0, equipped with an involution ω\omega which maps 𝔤 n\mathfrak{g}_n to 𝔤 −n\mathfrak{g}_{-n} and acts as −1-1 on 𝔤 0\mathfrak{g}_0, and with an ω\omega-invariant symmetric invariant bilinear form (−,−)(-,-) which pairs 𝔤 n\mathfrak{g}_n only with 𝔤 −n\mathfrak{g}_{-n}, such that the contravariant form

(x,y) 0≔−(x,ω(y)) (x,y)_0 \;\coloneqq\; -\big(x, \omega(y)\big)

is positive definite on all 𝔤 n≠0\mathfrak{g}_{n \neq 0}, then the kernel of (−,−)(-,-) is central and the quotient by it is a Borcherds algebra (Borcherds 1988, Thm. 3.1).

Conversely, Borcherds algebras satisfy these conditions, up to minor technical caveats (Borcherds 1995a, §1). This is just the structure carried by spaces of physical states in string theory, where positivity of the contravariant form is the content of the no-ghost theorem (Borcherds 1988, §5). This is how Borcherds algebras were found (Borcherds 1986) and why they keep appearing in string theory.

Definition

We follow Borcherds 1995a, §1. All Lie algebras are over the real numbers.

Definition

(Borcherds-Cartan matrix)

A Borcherds-Cartan matrix is a real square matrix A=(a ij) i,j∈IA = (a_{i j})_{i,j \in I}, indexed by a countable set II, such that:

  1. a ij=a jia_{i j} = a_{j i} for all i,j∈Ii, j \in I;

  2. a ij≤0a_{i j} \leq 0 for all i≠ji \neq j;

  3. if a ii>0a_{i i} \gt 0 then 2a ij/a ii∈ℤ2 a_{i j}/a_{i i} \in \mathbb{Z} for all j∈Ij \in I.

An index i∈Ii \in I is called real if a ii>0a_{i i} \gt 0, and imaginary if a ii≤0a_{i i} \leq 0 (isotropic if moreover a ii=0a_{i i} = 0).

Definition

(universal Borcherds algebra)

Given a Borcherds-Cartan matrix AA (Def. ), the universal Borcherds algebra 𝔤^(A)\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}(A) is the Lie algebra generated by elements

e i,f i,h ij,i,j∈I, e_i,\, f_i,\, h_{i j} \,, \;\;\;\; i, j \in I \,,

subject to the following relations, for all i,j,k∈Ii, j, k \in I:

  1. [e i,f j]=h ij[e_i, f_j] = h_{i j};

  2. [h ij,e k]=δ ija ike k[h_{i j}, e_k] = \delta_{i j}\, a_{i k}\, e_k and [h ij,f k]=−δ ija ikf k[h_{i j}, f_k] = - \delta_{i j}\, a_{i k}\, f_k;

  3. if a ii>0a_{i i} \gt 0 and j≠ij \neq i, then (ad e i) 1−2a ij/a ii(e j)=0(\mathrm{ad}_{e_i})^{1 - 2 a_{i j}/a_{i i}}(e_j) = 0 and (ad f i) 1−2a ij/a ii(f j)=0(\mathrm{ad}_{f_i})^{1 - 2 a_{i j}/a_{i i}}(f_j) = 0;

  4. if a ij=0a_{i j} = 0, then [e i,e j]=0[e_i, e_j] = 0 and [f i,f j]=0[f_i, f_j] = 0.

Remark

(the elements h ijh_{i j})

The relations of Def. imply that the h ijh_{i j} all commute with each other, that the h ijh_{i j} with i≠ji \neq j are central, and that h ij=0h_{i j} = 0 unless the iith and the jjth column of AA coincide (Borcherds 1991, see Gannon). Hence, if no two columns of AA coincide, then 𝔤^(A)\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}(A) is generated by the e ie_i, f if_i and h i≔h iih_i \coloneqq h_{i i} subject to

[h i,h j]=0,[e i,f j]=δ ijh i,[h i,e j]=a ije j,[h i,f j]=−a ijf j [h_i, h_j] = 0 \,, \;\;\; [e_i, f_j] = \delta_{i j}\, h_i \,, \;\;\; [h_i, e_j] = a_{i j}\, e_j \,, \;\;\; [h_i, f_j] = - a_{i j}\, f_j

together with items 3 and 4 of Def. . This is of the familiar form of the Chevalley-Serre presentation of Kac-Moody algebras. In general, the quotient of 𝔤^(A)\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}(A) by the h ijh_{i j} with i≠ji \neq j is what is often taken as the definition of the Borcherds algebra 𝔤(A)\mathfrak{g}(A) (e.g. Gannon). The h ijh_{i j} with i≠ji \neq j matter only for central extensions (Borcherds 1991, Borcherds 1995a, §1).

Definition

(Borcherds algebra)

A Borcherds algebra (or generalized Kac-Moody algebra) is a Lie algebra of the form

𝔤=𝔞⋊𝔡 \mathfrak{g} \;=\; \mathfrak{a} \rtimes \mathfrak{d}

where

  • 𝔞\mathfrak{a} is the quotient of a universal Borcherds algebra 𝔤^(A)\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}(A) (Def. ) by a subspace of its center,

  • 𝔡\mathfrak{d} is an abelian Lie algebra acting on 𝔞\mathfrak{a} by derivations such that all the e ie_i and f if_i are eigenvectors.

In other words, Borcherds algebras are obtained from universal Borcherds algebras by discarding part of the center and adjoining commuting outer derivations.

Remark

(roots, Weyl group and contravariant form)

Let Q≔⨁ i∈Iℤα iQ \coloneqq \bigoplus_{i \in I} \mathbb{Z}\, \alpha_i be the free abelian group on symbols α i\alpha_i (the simple roots), equipped with the bilinear form given by (α i,α j)≔a ij(\alpha_i, \alpha_j) \coloneqq a_{i j}. Declaring e ie_i to be of degree α i\alpha_i, f if_i of degree −α i-\alpha_i and h ijh_{i j} of degree 00 makes (universal) Borcherds algebras QQ-graded. A root is a nonzero α∈Q\alpha \in Q whose degree-α\alpha subspace is non-trivial. Every root is a non-negative or a non-positive integer combination of simple roots, and a root is called real if (α,α)>0(\alpha, \alpha) \gt 0 and imaginary otherwise (Borcherds 1988, §1). The root spaces are finite-dimensional, and the simple root spaces are 1-dimensional. Moreover:

  • for each i∈Ii \in I the elements e ie_i, h ih_i, f if_i span a copy of 𝔰𝔩(2,ℝ)\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R}) if a ii≠0a_{i i} \neq 0, and a copy of the 3-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra if a ii=0a_{i i} = 0;

  • the Weyl group is the group of automorphisms of QQ generated by the reflections α↦α−2(α,α i)a iiα i\alpha \mapsto \alpha - \frac{2 (\alpha, \alpha_i)}{a_{i i}} \alpha_i in the real simple roots α i\alpha_i only;

  • the Cartan involution ω\omega is given by ω(e i)=−f i\omega(e_i) = - f_i and ω(f i)=−e i\omega(f_i) = - e_i, hence ω(h i)=−h i\omega(h_i) = - h_i.

For the Borcherds algebras as in Borcherds 1988, §1 (with Cartan subalgebra a real vector space HH containing the h ih_i, equipped with a symmetric bilinear form with (h i,h j)=a ij(h_i, h_j) = a_{i j}), there is a unique invariant symmetric bilinear form extending that on HH. The corresponding contravariant form (x,y) 0≔−(x,ω(y))(x,y)_0 \coloneqq -(x, \omega(y)) is positive definite on all root spaces (Borcherds 1988, Cor. 2.5, whose proof is completed in Kac 1990, §11.13, see Borcherds 1995a, §3). This is the “almost positivity” from the Idea-section.

Remark

(variants of the definition)

  • (Kac-Moody algebras as special cases) If a ii>0a_{i i} \gt 0 for all i∈Ii \in I, then item 4 in Def. follows from item 3 (Borcherds 1988, §1). After rescaling to a ii=2a_{i i} = 2 (as below), the Borcherds algebras of Def. are then the central quotients, extended by derivations, of symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebras.

  • (normalization) Rescaling the iith row of a Borcherds-Cartan matrix by 2/a ii2/a_{i i} for all real ii turns it into a symmetrizable matrix with a ii=2a_{i i} = 2 for real ii, and with a ij∈ℤa_{i j} \in \mathbb{Z} whenever a ii=2a_{i i} = 2. This is the normalization convention used, e.g., in Gannon. Kac 1990, §11.13 moreover admits non-symmetrizable matrices, for which however there is no invariant bilinear form (Borcherds 1995a, §1). For symmetrizable matrices, Kac’s definition agrees essentially with Def. (by Kac 1990, Thm. 11.13.1, see Borcherds 1995a, §3).

  • (superalgebras) There is an evident generalization to Lie superalgebras, where some of the generators e ie_i, f if_i are odd. These Borcherds superalgebras (or generalized Kac-Moody superalgebras) were envisaged already in Borcherds 1988, §5 and developed in Ray 1995, Ray 2006. It is mostly Borcherds superalgebras which appear in supergravity (see the references below).

Examples

Lie algebras of physical string states

The archetypical examples of Borcherds algebras, which also motivated the concept, are Lie algebras of physical quantum states of the bosonic string (Frenkel 1985, Borcherds 1986, 1988, §5). They are constructed from vertex algebras as follows (for exposition see Gebert 1993, §3.4):

Definition

(Lie algebra of physical states)

Let VV be a vertex algebra with conformal vector, whose Virasoro modes we denote by L nL_n, and with an invariant symmetric bilinear form. Its spaces of physical states of weight kk are

P k≔{v∈V|L 0v=kv,L nv=0for alln>0}. P^k \;\coloneqq\; \big\{ v \in V \,\big\vert\, L_0 v = k\, v ,\; L_n v = 0 \;\text{for all}\; n \gt 0 \big\} \,.

Then L −1P 0⊂P 1L_{-1} P^0 \subset P^1, and the quotient P 1/L −1P 0P^1 / L_{-1} P^0 is a Lie algebra with bracket given by the zero-modes of the vertex operators, [u,v]≔u 0v[u, v] \coloneqq u_0 v (Borcherds 1986, see Borcherds 1992, §6). The bilinear form descends to an invariant form on this Lie algebra, and we write

𝔤(V)≔(P 1/L −1P 0)/ker(−,−) \mathfrak{g}(V) \;\coloneqq\; \big( P^1 / L_{-1} P^0 \big) \big/ \mathrm{ker}(-,-)

for its quotient by the kernel of this form (the “null states”).

In string-theoretic terms, P 1P^1 consists of the on-shell states of the chiral string, and the bracket [u,v]=u 0v[u,v] = u_0 v is the action of the charge u 0=∮Y(u,z)dzu_0 = \oint Y(u,z)\, d z of the weight-1 current corresponding to uu.

For VV of central charge 26, the critical value for the bosonic string, the no-ghost theorem (Goddard & Thorn 1972) identifies the physical states with “transversal” states. Concretely (Borcherds 1992, Thm. 5.1), suppose that WW is a vertex operator algebra of central charge 24 with positive definite invariant bilinear form, and that

V=W⊗V II 1,1 V \;=\; W \otimes V_{\mathrm{II}_{1,1}}

is its tensor product with the lattice vertex algebra of the even unimodular Lorentzian lattice II 1,1\mathrm{II}_{1,1} of rank 2. Then 𝔤(V)\mathfrak{g}(V) is graded by II 1,1\mathrm{II}_{1,1}, with root spaces

𝔤(V) r≃W 1−r 2/2,0≠r∈II 1,1, \mathfrak{g}(V)_r \;\simeq\; W_{1 - r^2/2} \,, \;\;\;\; 0 \neq r \in \mathrm{II}_{1,1} \,,

the subspaces of WW of L 0L_0-eigenvalue 1−r 2/21 - r^2/2. This identification is equivariant for any group of automorphisms of WW. In particular, the contravariant form on 𝔤(V)\mathfrak{g}(V) is then positive definite on all nonzero root spaces, so that 𝔤(V)\mathfrak{g}(V) is a Borcherds algebra by the characterization recalled above. More generally, for every even Lorentzian lattice LL, the Lie algebra 𝔤(V L)\mathfrak{g}(V_L) of physical states of the lattice vertex algebra V LV_L is a Borcherds algebra (Borcherds 1988, §5, Borcherds 1995a, Ex. 2).

The two most prominent examples are the following.

Example

(fake Monster Lie algebra)

The fake Monster Lie algebra is

𝔤(V II 25,1), \mathfrak{g}\big( V_{\mathrm{II}_{25,1}} \big) \,,

the Lie algebra of physical states of the lattice vertex algebra of the even unimodular Lorentzian lattice II 25,1\mathrm{II}_{25,1} of rank 26 (Borcherds 1990, where it is still called “the monster Lie algebra”). Example

(Monster Lie algebra)

The Monster Lie algebra is (Borcherds 1992, §6)

𝔪≔𝔤(V ♮⊗V II 1,1), \mathfrak{m} \;\coloneqq\; \mathfrak{g}\big( V^\natural \otimes V_{\mathrm{II}_{1,1}} \big) \,,

the Lie algebra of physical states for the moonshine module V ♮V^\natural (Frenkel, Lepowsky & Meurman 1988) tensored with the lattice vertex algebra of II 1,1≃ℤ 2\mathrm{II}_{1,1} \simeq \mathbb{Z}^2, with inner product ((m,n),(m′,n′))=−(mn′+m′n)\big((m,n),(m',n')\big) = -(m n' + m' n). Hence it is the Lie algebra of physical states of the chiral bosonic string on the product of the “Monster CFT” with a 2-dimensional Lorentzian torus.

Here V ♮=⨁ k≥0V k ♮V^\natural = \bigoplus_{k \geq 0} V^\natural_k is a vertex operator algebra of central charge 24 with positive definite invariant form, whose automorphism group is the monster group 𝕄\mathbb{M}, and whose graded dimension is the normalized j-invariant:

∑ k≥0dim(V k ♮)q k−1=j(q)−744=∑ n≥−1c(n)q n=q −1+196884q+21493760q 2+⋯. \sum_{k \geq 0} \mathrm{dim}\big( V^\natural_k \big)\, q^{k-1} \;=\; j(q) - 744 \;=\; \sum_{n \geq -1} c(n)\, q^n \;=\; q^{-1} + 196884\, q + 21493760\, q^2 + \cdots \,.

Therefore, by the no-ghost theorem (as above), 𝔪\mathfrak{m} is ℤ 2\mathbb{Z}^2-graded with root spaces

𝔪 (m,n)≃V 1+mn ♮for(m,n)≠(0,0) \mathfrak{m}_{(m,n)} \;\simeq\; V^\natural_{1 + m n} \;\;\; \text{for} \; (m,n) \neq (0,0)

as 𝕄\mathbb{M}-representations, hence of dimension c(mn)c(m n), while the Cartan subalgebra 𝔪 (0,0)\mathfrak{m}_{(0,0)} is 2-dimensional (Borcherds 1992, Thm. 6.1). The unique real simple root is (1,−1)(1,-1), of norm 2, and the imaginary simple roots are the (1,n)(1,n) for n≥1n \geq 1, of norm −2n-2n and with multiplicity c(n)c(n) (Borcherds 1992, Thm. 7.2). Accordingly, writing pp and qq for the formal exponentials of the degrees (1,0)(1,0) and (0,1)(0,1), the denominator identity of 𝔪\mathfrak{m} is the product formula

p −1∏ m>0,n∈ℤ(1−p mq n) c(mn)=j(p)−j(q). p^{-1} \prod_{m \gt 0, n \in \mathbb{Z}} \big(1 - p^m q^n\big)^{c(m n)} \;=\; j(p) - j(q) \,.

Since the Monster group acts on 𝔪\mathfrak{m} with root spaces the 𝕄\mathbb{M}-representations V 1+mn ♮V^\natural_{1 + m n}, there is for each g∈𝕄g \in \mathbb{M} a twisted denominator identity (Borcherds 1992, (8.3)):

p −1exp(−∑ i≥1∑ m≥1,n∈ℤTr(g i|V 1+mn ♮)p miq nii)=T g(p)−T g(q), p^{-1} \exp\Big( - \sum_{i \geq 1} \sum_{m \geq 1, n \in \mathbb{Z}} \mathrm{Tr}\big(g^i \,\vert\, V^\natural_{1 + m n}\big)\, \frac{p^{m i}\, q^{n i}}{i} \Big) \;=\; T_g(p) - T_g(q) \,,

where

T g(q)≔∑ n≥−1Tr(g|V n+1 ♮)q n T_g(q) \;\coloneqq\; \sum_{n \geq -1} \mathrm{Tr}\big(g \,\vert\, V^\natural_{n+1}\big)\, q^n

is the McKay-Thompson series of gg. For g=eg = e this reduces to the previous identity, since T e=j−744T_e = j - 744. These identities imply recursion relations (“replication formulas”) which determine each T gT_g from its first few coefficients. Checking these coefficients proves that the T gT_g are the Hauptmoduln predicted by Conway and Norton, hence the monstrous moonshine conjecture (Borcherds 1992, Thm. 1.1).

In both examples, the denominator identity is the product expansion of an automorphic form. For the fake Monster Lie algebra this is Borcherds’ automorphic form Φ 12\Phi_{12} of weight 12 for the orthogonal group of the lattice II 26,2\mathrm{II}_{26,2} (Borcherds 1995b). For the Monster Lie algebra it is j(p)−j(q)j(p) - j(q), a modular function in each of the variables pp and qq. More generally, the denominator identities of Borcherds algebras are often automorphic forms on orthogonal groups O(s+2,2)O(s+2,2) with infinite product expansions (“Borcherds products”, Borcherds 1995b, 1998). Conversely, hyperbolic Kac-Moody algebras may be enlarged (“automorphically corrected”) to Borcherds (super)algebras whose denominator function is an automorphic form (Gritsenko & Nikulin 1997).

Algebras of BPS States

In string theory, Borcherds algebras appear as algebras of BPS states (Harvey & Moore 1996, 1998). For instance, the degeneracies of dyons in 𝒩=4\mathcal{N}=4 string compactifications are given in terms of the denominator of a Borcherds superalgebra (Dijkgraaf, Verlinde & Verlinde 1997), whose Weyl group governs their wall crossing (Cheng & Verlinde 2008). The Monster Lie algebra itself appears as an algebra of BPS states in certain heterotic string models (Paquette, Persson & Volpato 2016).

Organizing SuGra higher gauge fields

In supergravity, Borcherds superalgebras organize the p-form higher gauge fields together with their duals, via the Bianchi identities of their flux densities.

The mechanism. The Bianchi identities of the flux densities in supergravity are all of the schematic form “dF=F∧Fd F = F \wedge F”. This means that they encode a Lie superalgebra (Cremmer, Julia, Lü & Pope 1998a, b). To see this, associate to each flux density F n XF^X_n of degree nn, hence to each potential of degree p=n−1p = n - 1, a generator e Xe_X of degree pp, which is even or odd according to the parity of pp. Then all the Bianchi identities combine into the single equation

d𝔽=12[𝔽,𝔽],𝔽≔∑ XF n Xe X, d \mathbb{F} \;=\; \frac{1}{2} [\mathbb{F}, \mathbb{F}] \,, \;\;\;\;\; \mathbb{F} \;\coloneqq\; \sum_X F^X_n\, e_X \,,

whose right-hand side defines a graded antisymmetric bracket on the generators. Consistency of the Bianchi identities means that applying dd once more makes the right-hand side vanish by itself. This is a cubic condition on the fluxes, and it is equivalent to the graded Jacobi identity for the bracket (Greitz & Howe 2011b, §1). In Cremmer, Julia, Lü & Pope 1998b this leads to a “doubled” formulation of maximal supergravities, in which the equations of motion become a twisted self-duality condition on the total field strength, valued in a finite-dimensional Lie superalgebra.

These finite-dimensional “duality superalgebras” turn out to be truncations of infinite-dimensional Borcherds superalgebras. For toroidal KK-compactifications of M-theory, the root lattices of these Borcherds superalgebras are the second integral cohomology lattices of del Pezzo surfaces (Henry-Labordère, Julia & Paulot 2002, 2003, cf. mysterious duality). These Borcherds superalgebras may also be derived from E 11 E_{11} (Henneaux, Julia & Levie 2012).

The truncation is a consequence of working on ordinary spacetime, where forms of degree greater than the spacetime dimension vanish. In superspace, on the other hand, the odd basis 1-forms commute with each other, so that there are non-vanishing forms of arbitrary degree. Accordingly, in the superspace formulation the algebra of all consistent flux densities becomes the full positive part 𝔫 +\mathfrak{n}^+ of the Borcherds superalgebra (Greitz & Howe 2011b). Moreover, a superspace cohomology argument shows two further facts (Greitz & Howe 2011b, §2):

  • once the Bianchi identities of the physical fields hold, those of all the higher-degree flux densities are automatically solvable;

  • there are no “gauge-trivial” flux densities (those with dF=0d F = 0) apart from exact 11-forms.

The argument uses the vanishing of the relevant cohomology groups H t p,qH^{p,q}_t of the dimension-zero torsion for p>1p \gt 1 (Berkovits & Howe 2008).

The case of type IIA supergravity illustrates the mechanism most transparently:

Example

(form algebra of type IIA supergravity)

The bosonic flux densities of (massless) type IIA supergravity are

  • the RR-fluxes G 2G_2, G 4G_4 and their duals G 6G_6, G 8G_8;

  • the NS-flux H 3H_3 (of the B-field), its dual H 7H_7, and the 9-flux H 9H_9 dual to the dilaton;

  • the RR 10-flux G 10G_{10}, dual to the Romans mass G 0G_0, and NS 11-fluxes H 11H_{11}, the field strengths of 10-form potentials.

With wedge products understood, their Bianchi identities read (Greitz & Howe 2011b, (2.13-14)):

dH 3 = 0 dG 2k+2 = H 3G 2k,k=0,⋯,4,G 0=0 dH 7 = 12G 4G 4−G 2G 6 dH 9 = −H 3H 7+12G 4G 6−32G 2G 8, \begin{array}{rcl} d H_3 &=& 0 \\ d G_{2k+2} &=& H_3 \, G_{2k} \mathrlap{\,, \;\;\; k = 0, \cdots, 4 \,, \;\;\; G_0 = 0 } \\ d H_7 &=& \frac{1}{2} G_4 \, G_4 - G_2 \, G_6 \\ d H_9 &=& - H_3 \, H_7 + \frac{1}{2} G_4 \, G_6 - \frac{3}{2} G_2 \, G_8 \,, \end{array}

together with a 2-parameter family of consistent Bianchi identities for H 11H_{11}. In the Romans-massive case, G 0=m≠0G_0 = m \neq 0, these are modified to dG 2=G 0H 3d G_2 = G_0 \, H_3, and in the NS identities G 2G 2nG_2 \, G_{2n} is replaced by G 2G 2n−G 0G 2n+2G_2\, G_{2n} - G_0 \, G_{2n+2} for n≥3n \geq 3 (Greitz & Howe 2011b, §2.2, see also Lavrinenko, Lü, Pope & Stelle 1999).

To find the corresponding Lie superalgebra (Greitz & Howe 2011b, §4.2), assign the even generator e 0e_0 to H 3H_3 and the odd generator e 1e_1 to G 2G_2. Then the e 0e_0-component of d𝔽=12[𝔽,𝔽]d \mathbb{F} = \frac{1}{2}[\mathbb{F}, \mathbb{F}] is

dH 3e 0=12G 2G 2[e 1,e 1], d H_3 \, e_0 \;=\; \frac{1}{2} G_2 \, G_2 \, [e_1, e_1] \,,

so that dH 3=0d H_3 = 0 requires [e 1,e 1]=0[e_1, e_1] = 0. Writing e 01≔[e 0,e 1]e_{01} \coloneqq [e_0, e_1], the generators of the higher fluxes are obtained by iterated brackets:

  • the RR-fluxes correspond to G 2k↔(ad e 0) k−1(e 1)G_{2k} \leftrightarrow (\mathrm{ad}_{e_0})^{k-1}(e_1), for all k≥1k \geq 1;

  • the further NS-fluxes arise from brackets of these, starting with H 7↔[e 01,e 01]H_7 \leftrightarrow [e_{01}, e_{01}] and H 9↔[e 0,[e 01,e 01]]H_9 \leftrightarrow [e_0, [e_{01}, e_{01}]].

The smallest Borcherds superalgebra (in the sense of Remark ) which accommodates these brackets has generators e i,h i,f ie_i, h_i, f_i for i∈{0,1}i \in \{0,1\}, with e 1e_1 and f 1f_1 odd, and Cartan matrix

(0 −1 −1 0). \left( \array{ 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 } \right) \,.

The entries are fixed as follows:

  • a 11=0a_{1 1} = 0 is forced by [e 1,e 1]=0[e_1, e_1] = 0;

  • a 00=0a_{0 0} = 0 is forced by the fact that (ad e 0) n(e 1)≠0(\mathrm{ad}_{e_0})^n(e_1) \neq 0 for all nn, while there are only finitely many forms of each degree: a 00>0a_{0 0} \gt 0 would contradict the first fact, and a 00<0a_{0 0} \lt 0 would produce a copy of 𝔰𝔩(2)\mathfrak{sl}(2) with infinite-dimensional representations, contradicting the second;

  • a 01=a 10=−1a_{0 1} = a_{1 0} = -1 is a normalization.

Both simple roots are isotropic, so that (e i,h i,f i)(e_i, h_i, f_i) generate Heisenberg (super)algebras.

The algebra of all IIA flux densities is isomorphic to the positive part 𝔫 +\mathfrak{n}^+ of this Borcherds superalgebra. A flux density of degree 2n+m+12n + m + 1 corresponds to a root nα 0+mα 1n \alpha_0 + m \alpha_1 as follows (Greitz & Howe 2011b, §4.2):

In particular, the multiplicity 2 at n=2n = 2 matches the two independent 11-flux and 13-flux Bianchi identities found in the superspace analysis (Greitz & Howe 2011b, (2.15), (3.8)).

Beyond the spacetime limit, most of these higher fluxes vanish in supergravity, but not all of them. The RR 12-flux G 12G_{12}, corresponding to the root 5α 0+α 15 \alpha_0 + \alpha_1, is non-vanishing: its component with ten even and two odd superspace indices is a non-zero multiple of e −ϕΓ 10,2e^{-\phi}\, \Gamma_{10,2}, and its Bianchi identity dG 12=H 3G 10d G_{12} = H_3 \, G_{10} is automatically solved (Greitz & Howe 2011b, (3.7)). The NS 13-fluxes vanish identically, but their Bianchi identities have non-vanishing terms on the right, whose sum vanishes by the cohomology argument. It has been speculated that such higher-degree forms may become non-zero once string-theoretic higher-order corrections are included (Greitz & Howe 2011b, §5).

Remark

(flux densities as closed 𝔫 +\mathfrak{n}^+-valued forms)

Grade 𝔫 +\mathfrak{n}^+ by the potential degree pp, so that in the IIA case p(α 0)=2p(\alpha_0) = 2 and p(α 1)=1p(\alpha_1) = 1, whence the super-parity of each root space is pp mod 2. This makes 𝔫 +\mathfrak{n}^+ a ℤ \mathbb{Z} -graded Lie algebra concentrated in positive degrees, hence an L-infinity algebra with only a binary bracket. Up to the sign convention 𝔽↦−𝔽\mathbb{F} \mapsto - \mathbb{F}, the combined Bianchi identity d𝔽=12[𝔽,𝔽]d \mathbb{F} = \frac{1}{2}[\mathbb{F}, \mathbb{F}] then says that the flux densities constitute a closed 𝔫 + \mathfrak{n}^+ -valued differential form, hence a dg-algebra homomorphism

CE(𝔫 +)⟶Ω dR •(X) \mathrm{CE}\big(\mathfrak{n}^+\big) \longrightarrow \Omega^\bullet_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)

out of the Chevalley-Eilenberg algebra of 𝔫 +\mathfrak{n}^+. In the CE algebra, the generators dual to root spaces of potential degree pp sit in degree p+1p+1. This is the form in which Bianchi identities enter flux quantization.

For example, in the IIA case the root spaces whose roots have α 1\alpha_1-coefficient ≥2\geq 2 span an ideal in 𝔫 +\mathfrak{n}^+. The quotient by this ideal retains only H 3H_3 and the RR-fluxes G 2kG_{2k}, with their Bianchi identities dH 3=0d H_3 = 0 and dG 2k+2=H 3G 2kd G_{2k+2} = H_3 \, G_{2k}, which are those of the twisted Chern character in twisted K-theory.

Example

(form algebra of type IIB supergravity)

All flux densities of type IIB supergravity have odd degree, so that its Borcherds algebra is purely even. Its Cartan matrix is

(0 −1 −1 2), \left( \array{ 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 } \right) \,,

which gives the rank-2 Borcherds algebra previously considered by Slansky 1993 (Henry-Labordère, Julia & Paulot 2002, Greitz & Howe 2011b, §4.1). Its two simple roots play the following roles:

  • the real simple root α 1\alpha_1 generates a copy of 𝔰𝔩(2)\mathfrak{sl}(2), the (complexified) Lie algebra of the U-duality group SL(2,ℝ)SL(2,\mathbb{R}), with e 1e_1 associated with the axion;

  • the isotropic simple root α 0\alpha_0 corresponds to the lowest weight of the doublet of 3-fluxes.

Flux densities of degree 2n+12n+1 sit at level nn, the coefficient of α 0\alpha_0. Up to level 7 their 𝔰𝔩(2)\mathfrak{sl}(2)-content is

2,1,2,3,4+2,5+3+1,6+2⋅4+2⋅2. \mathbf{2} ,\;\; \mathbf{1} ,\;\; \mathbf{2} ,\;\; \mathbf{3} ,\;\; \mathbf{4} + \mathbf{2} ,\;\; \mathbf{5} + \mathbf{3} + \mathbf{1} ,\;\; \mathbf{6} + 2 \cdot \mathbf{4} + 2 \cdot \mathbf{2} \,.

This matches, including the multiplicities of the 15-fluxes, the consistent Bianchi identities found in superspace (Greitz & Howe 2011b, §3.1, §4.1).

Analogous statements hold in other dimensions:

  • For maximal supergravity in DD dimensions, 3≤D≤113 \leq D \leq 11, the dynamical pp-forms correspond to (a truncation of) a real Borcherds superalgebra whose Cartan matrix is given explicitly for each DD, and which may be derived from E 11 E_{11} (Henneaux, Julia & Levie 2012, see also Kleinschmidt & Palmkvist 2013).

  • The superspace analysis of form algebras has been carried out for (half-)maximal supergravity in 3≤D≤113 \leq D \leq 11 (Howe & Palmkvist 2015; for D=3D = 3 see Greitz & Howe 2011a, 2012). There, the resulting Borcherds superalgebras are shown to yield the same form spectrum as associated Kac-Moody algebras, which is E 11E_{11} in the maximal case (see also Palmkvist 2012b).

  • Gauging deforms the Bianchi identities by the embedding tensor, which extends the form algebra to a tensor hierarchy algebra (Palmkvist 2014, Greitz, Howe & Palmkvist 2014, Howe & Palmkvist 2015). In this way Borcherds superalgebras encode the tensor hierarchies of gauged supergravity (Palmkvist 2012a).

  • Borcherds superalgebras also govern the gauge structure of exceptional generalized geometry (Palmkvist 2015), in the form of L ∞ L_\infty -algebras (Cederwall & Palmkvist 2019, Lavau & Palmkvist 2020).
