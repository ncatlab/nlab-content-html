Higher curvature corrections to 11D supergravity

Higher curvature corrections to 11D supergravity

Eleven-dimensional supergravity is meant to describe the long-wavelength dynamics of the graviton, the three-form C 3C_3 and the gravitino of M-theory. The first local higher curvature correction visible in flat-space graviton scattering has eight derivatives. Two features of this correction are particularly well established. The four-graviton scattering amplitude fixes a quartic-curvature coupling, and cancellation of the gravitational anomaly of an M5-brane requires a coupling C 3∧I 8(R)C_3\wedge I_8(R) with a definite coefficient. What remains only partly known is the nonlinear supersymmetric interaction of all three fields on a general background. (Chester, Pufu & Yin, §1, Duff, Liu & Minasian, §3, Souères & Tsimpis, §§1, 4–5, Aggarwal et al., §§1, 5)

What is the expansion in the Planck length?

Consider a process of wavelength LL, or a weakly curved background of curvature radius LL. A Riemann tensor counts as two derivatives. An interaction ℓ p 6R 4\ell_p^6 R^4, where R 4R^4 stands for contractions of four Riemann tensors, therefore contributes at relative order (ℓ p/L) 6(\ell_p/L)^6 compared with the Einstein term. The low-energy limit is L≫ℓ pL\gg\ell_p, equivalently Eℓ p≪1E\ell_p\ll1 in units ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1.

Uncompactified M-theory is expected to have “no dimensionless coupling constant”. One can set ℓ p\ell_p equal to one by choosing units and still perform experiments at different energies in those units. The numbers multiplying independent interactions are data of the theory, to be determined by matching. Restoring ℏ\hbar, with c=1c=1, gives ℓ p 9∝ℏG 11\ell_p^9\propto\hbar G_{11}, where G 11G_{11} is Newton’s constant. A correction proportional to ℓ p 6\ell_p^6 thus disappears in the classical limit at fixed G 11G_{11} and LL. A classical gravity theory with an independently specified length could contain the same curvature polynomial. (Chester, Pufu & Yin, §1 and footnote 9)

Compactification adds a scale. On a circle of radius R 11R_{11}, the ratio R 11/ℓ p=g s 2/3R_{11}/\ell_p=g_s^{2/3}, in the usual Planck-length convention, determines the string coupling constant of type IIA string theory. Varying this modulus varies the ten-dimensional coupling. (Witten, §2)

In what sense are the corrections quantum?

  • The ten-dimensional string calculation. The type IIA interaction B 2∧I 8(R)B_2\wedge I_8(R) occurs at genus one of the closed string. This is the origin of the name one-loop term for I 8 I_8 . At the same order α′ 3\alpha'^3 relative to the Einstein term, there is already a gravitational R 4R^4 interaction at genus zero. Powers of α′E 2\alpha' E^2 count derivatives, while each additional handle of the worldsheet costs a power of g s 2g_s^2. In the eleven-dimensional decompactification limit the IIA tree contribution is suppressed and the genus-one combination survives as a local 11D interaction. (Vafa & Witten, Gross & Witten, Souères & Tsimpis, (1.1)–(1.6))

  • The eleven-dimensional anomaly argument. Starting with the classical supergravity action, one can ask which extra interactions preserve local supersymmetry. That question alone leaves the overall coefficient of a first-order deformation free. Additional physical input comes from quantizing the chiral fields on a magnetic five-brane. Their gravitational anomaly requires anomaly inflow with a fixed normalization once the five-brane charge is specified. The quantum anomaly arises on the six-dimensional worldvolume. The corresponding inflow comes from varying the classical bulk coupling C 3∧I 8(R)C_3\wedge I_8(R) in the presence of the brane. The cubic bulk coupling also contributes to cancellation of the normal-bundle anomaly. On circle reduction, the component of C 3C_3 with one leg along the circle gives B 2B_2, reproducing the type IIA curvature coupling. (Duff, Liu & Minasian, §3, Hořava & Witten, discussion following (1.14), Freed et al.)

  • Loops of the eleven-dimensional bulk fields. With a momentum cutoff Λ\Lambda, the one-loop four-graviton amplitude of two-derivative supergravity contains a local term proportional to Λ 3t 8t 8R 4\Lambda^3 t_8t_8R^4. Its finite coefficient requires matching. On a circle this same 11D loop also gives a finite, radius-dependent term which reproduces the type IIA tree-level four-graviton coupling. Even in uncompactified space one can distinguish two orders experimentally in principle. In the amplitude divided by its tree value, the matched local R 4R^4 term is of order (Eℓ p) 6(E\ell_p)^6, while the nonanalytic threshold from a loop of massless supergravity fields is of order (Eℓ p) 9(E\ell_p)^9 at fixed scattering angle. Thus a power of ℓ p\ell_p labels an order in energy. Assigning a loop order requires specifying which fields are being quantized. (Green, Gutperle & Vanhove, (10)–(11), Chester, Pufu & Yin, (1.1)–(1.3))

There is a close analogue in 5-dimensional supergravity. Local supersymmetry gives a completion of A∧tr(R∧R)A\wedge\operatorname{tr}(R\wedge R). Integrating out charged massive fields gives a one-loop shift of its coefficient. One calculation determines how the interaction fits into supergravity. The other determines what a specified spectrum contributes to it. (Hanaki, Ohashi & Tachikawa, Bonetti, Grimm & Hohenegger)

What is known at eight derivatives?

  • Four gravitons. The first local correction to the four-graviton amplitude, divided by the tree amplitude of two-derivative supergravity, is ℓ 11 6stu/(3⋅2 7)\ell_{11}^6 s t u/(3\cdot 2^7) in the conventions of Chester, Pufu & Yin. Here s,t,us,t,u are the Mandelstam invariants and 2κ 11 2=(2π) 5ℓ 11 92\kappa_{11}^2=(2\pi)^5\ell_{11}^9 in their length convention. They recover this coefficient independently from a correlation function of the ABJM model. In a covariant action the purely gravitational terms can be represented, up to field redefinitions and overall normalization, by t 8t 8R 4−14!ϵ 11ϵ 11R 4t_8t_8R^4-\frac{1}{4!}\epsilon_{11}\epsilon_{11}R^4 in the conventions of Souères & Tsimpis. The four-graviton amplitude sees the t 8t 8t_8t_8 contraction. The leading expansion of the double-epsilon term is a total derivative, so it first contributes to graviton scattering at five points. (Chester, Pufu & Yin, (1.1)–(1.3) and footnote 9, Souères & Tsimpis, (1.5)–(1.6))

  • One three-form and four gravitons. The characteristic form in the inflow coupling is I 8(R)=148(p 2(R)−14p 1(R) 2)I_8(R)=\frac{1}{48}\big(p_2(R)-\frac14 p_1(R)^2\big) in the convention of I8. Its first vertex about flat space with vanishing background flux has five external fields. An eleven-dimensional superparticle calculation recovers its tensor structure and leaves the ultraviolet normalization to be supplied separately. (Anguelova, Grassi & Vanhove, §6.2, especially (6.27))

  • Flux and gravitini. Writing G 4G_4 for the four-form field strength, the bosonic four-point interactions include R 2(∇G 4) 2R^2(\nabla G_4)^2 and (∇G 4) 4(\nabla G_4)^4. Their circle reduction has been checked against type IIA string amplitudes, including amplitudes with Ramond–Ramond fields. Component supersymmetry has also fixed particular gravitino bilinears and constrained five-field terms of type R 3G 4 2R^3G_4^2. As Aggarwal et al. put it, “At higher points there are only partial results”. (Peeters, Plefka & Stern, Aggarwal et al., §§1, 3–5, Peeters, Vanhove & Westerberg, Hyakutake 2007, Hyakutake 2025)

An earlier power of ℓ p\ell_p occurs in the study of flux quantization. In the four-form deformation complex, the groups at orders ℓ p\ell_p and ℓ p 2\ell_p^2 vanish. At order ℓ p 3\ell_p^3 a class is represented by a shift of G 4G_4 proportional to ℓ p 3p 1(R)\ell_p^3 p_1(R). Locally one can absorb it by shifting C 3C_3 by a Lorentz Chern–Simons form. Globally it can change the flux quantization law. This accounts for the order ℓ p 3\ell_p^3 in Tsimpis’s analysis. The quartic-curvature interaction in graviton scattering occurs at order ℓ p 6\ell_p^6. (Tsimpis, (4.11)–(4.18), discussion at I 8I_8)

The anomaly-corrected seven-form Bianchi identity

Away from brane sources, the corrected bosonic C 3C_3 equation can be written to this order as

dG 4=0,dG 7+12G 4∧G 4=βI 8(R),β=O(ℓ p 6). \mathrm{d}G_4=0,\qquad \mathrm{d}G_7+\tfrac12 G_4\wedge G_4=\beta I_8(R), \qquad \beta=O(\ell_p^6).

Here the signs are those of the flux equation at I 8I_8, and β\beta includes the normalization of the fluxes. The gauge-invariant interactions involving G 4G_4 also contribute to this equation, through the corrected duality relation G 7=⋆G 4+O(ℓ p 6)G_7=\star G_4+O(\ell_p^6). A measurable consequence in a compactification on a smooth compact Calabi–Yau fourfold YY is that ∫ YI 8=χ(Y)/24\int_Y I_8=\chi(Y)/24 contributes to the membrane tadpole, constraining the net M2-brane charge and the four-form flux. (Souères & Tsimpis, (4.2)–(4.11), Sethi, Vafa & Witten, Becker & Becker, Witten, §3)

What remains to be constructed in superspace?

On superspace the curvature source has components with spinor indices. The flux and torsion Bianchi identities must hold in those components as well. First order here means first order in the coefficient β\beta. Even at first order the answer may depend nonlinearly on all the fields. There are three distinct questions.

  1. Does the anomaly coupling belong to a first-order superinvariant, and when is it unique? Howe & Tsimpis qualify their uniqueness argument with “Provided that this equation is consistent”. They also assume that the invariant begins at least quartically in the fields. Souères & Tsimpis give a sufficient condition for constructing it. Let X 0,8X_{0,8} denote the component with eight spinor indices of their anomaly superform X 8X_8, proportional to the superspace I 8I_8. If X 0,8X_{0,8} is exact for the algebraic differential τ\tau induced by the dimension-zero torsion, then G 4 (0)∧X 8G_4^{(0)}\wedge X_8 has a gauge-invariant superform primitive on undeformed on-shell superspace. The superform action principle then gives the invariant. Of three potentially obstructing Spin(1,10)\mathrm{Spin}(1,10) representations, they verify the cancellation of (04000)(04000) and (02004)(02004). Their paper leaves the (03002)(03002) cancellation unchecked. The quartic hypothesis matters for uniqueness. Assuming a perturbative solution of the Bianchi identities, they identify at least two independent eight-derivative invariants when lower powers of the fields are allowed. Only one has the required anomaly coupling. (Howe & Tsimpis, §5, following (56), Souères & Tsimpis, §§4.1–4.2, 5 and Appendix C)

  2. What are the actual corrected geometry and field equations? In the four-form formulation a first-order deformation is encoded by [G 0,4 (1)]∈H F 0,4(phys)[G^{(1)}_{0,4}]\in H_F^{0,4}(\mathrm{phys}). The subscripts count vector and spinor indices, and the coefficients are local expressions in the supergravity fields and their derivatives. Given a representative, the Bianchi identities relate it to the change in dimension-zero torsion and hence to the field equations. Cederwall, Gran, Nilsson & Tsimpis solve the geometric identities to first order for general torsion-deformation tensors. To obtain the explicit M-theory equations one must still construct the particular tensors selected by the anomaly and extract their component couplings, including the gravitino terms. (Howe & Tsimpis, §§4, 6, Cederwall, Gran, Nilsson & Tsimpis, §3 and Appendix B, details at I 8I_8)

  3. Does the deformation extend to higher orders? Already at second order the first-order change in the supersymmetry transformations acts on the first-order action. Its variation has to be cancelled by further local corrections. Each order presents a compatibility question. Independent invariants can also first occur at higher derivative orders, with coefficients requiring their own determination. The November 2026 nLab working materials describe a proposed continuation of the I 8I_8 deformation with its leading coefficient kept symbolic. Their overview reports a second-order result under stated hypotheses on the local complex, comparison maps, sources and extensions. It lists compatibility at arbitrary order as open. (Howe & Tsimpis, §7, Souères & Tsimpis, §4.3 and footnote 2)

What would the full tower determine?

Particular observables are known beyond eight derivatives. In the uncompactified four-graviton amplitude the coefficient of D 4R 4D^4R^4 vanishes and the coefficient of D 6R 4D^6R^4 is known. Here DD denotes a covariant derivative. Numerical conformal bootstrap calculations have also found evidence in ABJM data for the scaling expected from the unprotected D 8R 4D^8R^4 interaction. It is possible to know a later four-point coefficient while higher-point couplings at an earlier order are still being worked out. (Binder, Chester & Pufu, Green & Vanhove, Chester, Dempsey & Pufu, §3.2)

Knowing all local interactions of the massless multiplet, with their physical coefficients up to field redefinitions, would supply the vertices for low-energy scattering to every derivative order. Loops of the light fields then supply nonlocal contributions such as massless thresholds. On compactification one could calculate corrections to flux backgrounds and compare lower-dimensional couplings with string amplitudes, including the effects of Kaluza–Klein modes where appropriate. Even the leading correction changes the geometry of supersymmetric compactifications of special holonomy. (Chester, Pufu & Yin, §1, Green, Gutperle & Vanhove, Lü, Pope, Stelle & Townsend)

At energies of order 1/ℓ p1/\ell_p the derivative expansion loses its ordering by size. An ultraviolet completion would have to define observables there and account for the nonperturbative states and dynamics, including those of membranes and five-branes. The global quantization of the CC-field also requires information beyond a local Lagrangian density. The next question would be which microscopic dynamics reproduce the entire low-energy expansion and determine observables beyond it. The recovery of the R 4R^4 coefficient from ABJM gives a concrete example of how such a proposal can be tested. (Witten on flux quantization, Diaconescu, Freed & Moore, Banks et al. on matrix theory, Chester, Pufu & Yin)
